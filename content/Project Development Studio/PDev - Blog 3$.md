## Project Proposal

"Give a man a fish and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish and you feed him for a lifetime."

**$10,000 & 10 months**

With $10,000 and 10 months, I would build an infrastructure that knows the status of every machine in the shop. Some shops already use traffic lights (red, yellow and green) to show machine status. I would go further and place a robot in each section of the shop. The robots would share an understanding of how the tools work and could sense what each machine is doing.

The sensing is there so the robots know when to step in, not so they can do the work for you. A robot near the laser cutters could see that a job is set up wrong. Instead of fixing the file, it would ask: "What power setting did you choose for this material? What do you think will happen?" A robot by the 3D printers, connected to the Bambu Lab cameras, would notice a failed print and ask the user to look at it together: "Where did it start to fail? What was holding up that part?" It would guide them toward a better design rather than redesigning it for them. In the wood shop, a robot would ask, "Before you start, do you know where the miter saw's lock pin is?" When someone finishes at the saws, it would ask, "What's left to do before you leave this station?"

There is one exception: safety. If someone is about to get hurt, or a fire starts in a laser cutter, the robot doesn't ask questions. It warns, it stops and it calls for help. Everything else is a chance to learn.

AI is very good at doing things for us, but it can be just as good at teaching us: showing us the way and taking us places we thought we couldn't reach. This is the future of work as I imagine it: a technology that helps us learn to make what we want, instead of replacing us and taking over all the labor (just some of it).

---

**$10 & 10 days**

The heart of my project is using a personified AI to help people make things by guiding them in the right direction without handing them the answers. This is how I wish we used AI more.

With $10, I would buy a small keychain speaker (the kind sold as a kids' toy) and hack it to connect to an agent running locally on my computer. A user would press the button, say which part of the shop they're in and ask a question. The AI would then answer or suggest a way to work more smoothly and safely.

Looking at both extremes, the most important part of the project survives even in the $10 version: a voice that answers only when asked and that makes you explain your problem before it helps. What the $10,000 version adds is mostly sensing and infrastructure, not teaching. So my actual project sits closer to the small end: one robot, one character, built on Reachy Mini. The question I need to answer is what having a body adds that a keychain can't, such as presence, being approachable and something you can carry to the machine.
