## Key points that rose from desk critique:

1. **Having a case**  - Whatever form I choose, I need a clear case for it: an argument I can defend, not just a preference.

2. **Why are people going in a soft direction in robot design?** - Pedro’s question to me: there’s a reason the industry has moved toward soft, rounded robots, and I should understand it before going against it.

3. **Desirable robot** - The robot should be something people want to be near and interact with, not just something that functions.

4. **Round = more approachable** - Pedro came back to the same point: rounded forms make people more willing to approach.

Ive read [[Shape Symbolism in the Design of Socially Interactive Robots Rounded vs. Angular Contours]] A perception study asking whether rounded vs. angular contours change what people expect a robot to be and do, and how that interacts with humanoid vs. non-humanoid appearance. The hypothesis: rounded reads as friendly and companion-like, angular as potent and service-like.
