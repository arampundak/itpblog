## Prototyping Workshop

While some might think that prototypes are all about models and early samples, the core value of prototyping is in the actions, interactions, and questions that it might produce.

> _"Prototypes provide the means for examining design problems and evaluating solutions. Selecting the focus of a prototype is the art of identifying the most important open design questions."_
> 
> Houde and Hill - What do Prototypes Prototype?

**Prototype Scope**  
As a tool for communicating and testing ideas and processes, prototypes required a clear scope and setting.

- What do you want to test?
- Who are you showing this to?
- How can you evaluate your progress?

**Dimensions of Prototypes**

- looks-like: fidelity in appearance
- works-like: fidelity in behavior
- feels-like: fidelity in experience

**Physical Prototypes**  
While prototypes can be anything from diagrams to virtual reality spaces; physical models or 3D sketches tend to be great options to test new aesthetics, interactions, scale and concepts.

> _3D Sketch Model: Simple physical models made of soft, low cost, easy-to-work materials._

**3D Sketching (Physical Model)**  
When prototyping tangible experiences or physical objects, having a physical model can allow for deeper understanding and reflection of your design problems. In addition, it usually tends to provide more genuine feedbacks.

> _If shape is the opposite of "matter", then there is no design that can be called "material": it is always informative. And if the shape is the "how" of the matter, and "matter" is the "what" of shape, then the design is one of the methods of giving matter shape and making it look thus, and not different._
> 
> Vilem Flusser, Form und Material - Vienna 1991

- form: focus on shape embodiment
- scale: focus on properties such as size or mass
- visualization: focus on communication
- operational principles: focus on tech and physical behavior
- system configuration: focus on “what are the bits?”
- integration: focus on “do these bits play well together?”
- interaction: a focus on the use model
- usability: a focus on user understanding
- experience: a focus on the user’s feeling

## We don't make enough prototypes

- We skip prototyping to go down the fast lane. AI is removing friction, and things get lost in the process.
- A prototype is not an excuse for an early sample.
- The core value is not the physical artifact but the action, question, and interactions.
- A prototype is about asking a question, not about the thing.
- Prototype scope:
    - What are we testing?
    - Who are we showing it to?
    - How can we evaluate our progress?
- Dimensions of a prototype:
    - Looks-like: fidelity in appearance
    - Works-like: fidelity in behavior
    - Feels-like: fidelity in experience
- If you don’t have a way to assess your prototype, it’s not a prototype. Evaluate!
- What are the points of conflict?
- Making and prototyping are not the same!
- Ask different questions in different shapes.
- Build looks-like and works-like in parallel.
- Showed slides of prototypes, discussing size, tactility, shape, connectivity, and showing different questions.
- A prototype is built to move on from. You don’t need to like it. It’s a 3D sketch.
- If you build it over a long time, you start to care. Prototypes should be easy and multiple.
- The prototype is the process; the object is the sketch model.
- Use soft, low-cost, easy-to-work materials.
