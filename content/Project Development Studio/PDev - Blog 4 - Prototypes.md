In order to develop my project I made 3 types of prototypes. 
An inspiration caught my eye from the new Teenage Engineering concept store that opened in NYC. 
![[TE - absolute choir 2.webp|300]]

It shows a glimpse of their first project, the Absolute (vodka) choir.

![[TE - absolute choir.jpg]]

The boxy, drawer-like totem made me imagine the robot in a very simple shape. Like a wooden box.

![[mockup 1 sketches.webp]]

After sketching and knowing where I want to be I started with my #1 Mockup - The 3D file ill be working on. I had several questions about the internal components and assembly features, together with needing a file I could tear apart, build and rebuild.
I found all of [[Reachy mini]] parts as separate 3D files. I asked LLM to use the robots URDF (Unified Robot Description Format is an XML-based markup language developed for ROS) to reconstruct the assembly so I can use it as a base in Fusion360. 
To my shock - it did exactly that and more, which taught me new features of both programs.

![[pdev - claude builds reachy mini.mp4]]

After having the 3D model I could cross investigate its inner constructions while also looking at the manual by Pollen Robotics. With knowing its internal components and sizes I started measuring the new outer shell ill be designing.

My #2 mockup was for seeing what I drew and imagined in 3D - IRL. I asked myself how does it feel to sit next to these faced boxes, will it feel native to the shop? how is it compered to the rounded shapes of the original Reachy Mini?
![[pdev - mockup looks.webp|500]]

My #3 mockup is a functional inquiry about carrying the robot in the shop.
I made it much quicker and bulkier, glued its face and speaker vents as a 2D print on paper and added weights inside to balance while carrying. I used a simple wooden dowel to test the lantern style grip.
![[pdev - mockup handle.webp|600]]
