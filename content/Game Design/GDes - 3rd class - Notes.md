**Availability bias and game design**
probability vs expectation - in Settlers of Catan we through 2 dies. The player settling on the number we got gets the supply. In a streak of 3 twelves - we believe 12 will happen very often, though statistically it can't.
Perceived vs Actual risks is what availability bias is all about: Perceived risk is how people feel about a danger, while actual risk is the true statistical chance of that danger happening.

**Loss Aversion**
is a cognitive bias where the pain of a loss hurts about twice as much as the pleasure of an equivalent gain feels good.

**Prospect Theory**
You feel 2 times the pain because of losing, then you fill uplifted by winning.
- Reference points are critical to how we experience gains and losses, where we begin is very important.
- Doubling the amount we have is more meaningful then increasing it, making 200 out of a 100 feels better then gaining 100 over 900.
- We are driven more strongly to avoid losses then achieve gains.
- People prefer sure gains over a chance for larger gains.

**Endowment Effect**
The endowment effect is a psychological bias where people place a higher value on things they own than they would if they did not own them.

Encourage player to use things and not hoard them, how to spend resources, you can force a player to spend mana to cast a spell or you can flip it and have a resource pool to save them from something negative.
