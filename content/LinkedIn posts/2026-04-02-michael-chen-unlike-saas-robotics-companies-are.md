## Full text

Unlike SaaS, robotics companies are notorious for wanting to rebuild everything from scratch.

While AI SaaS companies are growing faster than ever by moving higher up the stack, taking advantage of thousands of packages, building on top of open-source modules, and focusing only on their specialty, robotics companies often spend their first year fumbling to rebuild the entire stack.

While ROS exists, most companies hate it and end up building their own middleware, communication protocols, and data visualization tools.

Even physical AI companies often build their own data collection pipelines, storage formats, data annotation tools, teleoperation hardware, everything they possibly can.

Companies come up with excuses that existing solutions aren’t “good enough”: not low-latency enough, not fast enough, or whatever the reason may be.

Yondu has always been built on the thesis that we stand on the shoulders of giants and that we share what we build so others can build on top of us.

That’s why I was so excited when Professor Kim and Aaron Park came to visit our office.

I showed them the various humanoid robots we use, our teleoperation tools, and our progress on autonomy.

Professor Kim shared his incredible work on an open-source teleoperation system that works across any robot form factor.

It’s energizing to share our work with others in the robotics field and help push the industry forward as a whole.

It’s also why, at Yondu, we focus on being hardware-agnostic and building internal tools that the broader robotics community can build upon.

We have a big announcement coming soon on our first internal tool for VR teleoperation: for humanoid robots and general-purpose robotics.
