## Full text

Robots are super hyped right now, but there is a unique catch 22 for building humanoid robots ♾️ 

Let's compare building an autonomous robot to building a self-driving car (i.e. road robot). To build an autopilot for a car, there is one key advantage: cars are already on the road, being driven by humans. Critical to building the auto-pilot for the car is collecting the data from the driver (in addition to collecting data from the car itself).

The problem for humanoids, unlike cars, is that there are no robots out there that are "driven" by humans doing useful things. Without that we have no data from the "driver" to train the robot to become autonomous. 

The first to come to mind is that to fix that it is enough to collect recordings of humans interacting in the world, and build an AI to simulate those recordings in a robot that looks like those humans. But doing that is like filming the car driving on the road, but without the data from the driver: 
No robot driver -> no data

One key solution to that is building what's called "tele-operating" tools. A teleoperating system makes it possible to have the robot out there doing stuff, while being "driven" by a human remotely. 

In that, we start to collect the data of what the human is doing (speech, eye movements, body movements, etc.) and train AI that can use the driver's data to build an AI that step-by-step replaces the driver. One of our tools is a real-time head and face control system, driven in the video by our Charlie Caper 💫 

We've been building those tools at Furhat from the get go, and hundreds of people are using them to put robots in the "wild", tele-operate them, and work on mapping the "driver" behaviour to AI models. One of those tools is in the video.
