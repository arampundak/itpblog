## Full text

What does it take to get front page in the New York Times? Short answer: build something that changes the world. For ElliQ, the companion robot for aging adults, the process to secure upper fold in the Sunday edition took a decade.

I was fortunate to help with the initial launch of ElliQ back in 2017 when I was Head of Communications at fuseproject. We had finished the upfront design process, their AI interface was functional, and we were ready to show the prototype to the world. 

Coincidentally, the Design Museum of London was curating an exhibition on design and aging, and we were able to leverage this show to give the media a first look at the robot.

In 2018 when I launched my executive thought leadership agency, Core Narratives, I continued working with Intuition Robotics to help define the narrative around technology and aging. Dor Skuler was an extraordinary partner in that he deeply cares about the impact of his work. This wasn't about selling a product -- he fundamentally wants to help aging adults feel less lonely.

The truth is, a robot may not be the ideal solution for this: in a perfect world, aging adults would have human social companionship and community support. But this simply is not the world we live in. In our research at the time, the average adult over the age of 70 who lives alone has 1 human interaction per week. 

What I love about ElliQ is that she is not meant to appear human. She looks almost like a Pixar character; a joyful tabletop figure that proactively suggests activities to keep users mentally active and engaged.

A normal company would have launched this product years earlier. But Intuition Robotics knew that putting a robot into someone's home is not insignificant, and it had to be perfect. They spent years refining their AI, their user experience (which won a Fast Company Innovation award), and testing with users before finally releasing the product.

Now we are seeing the impacts of this work. Jan is one of thousands of users who are experiencing some level of companionship and daily conversation with ElliQ. 

This is a product that shows what is possible in robotics and AI. It's not a gimmick, it's not trying to replace humans or save corporations money -- this is using technology to fill a gap that humans have been unable to fill, in order to fundamentally improve lives.

That's how you get front page of The New York Times. Build something that makes an impact. Even if it takes ten years.
