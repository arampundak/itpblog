## Full text

An electric motor in the form of a fiber? 🧵⚡ Imagine the design freedom this could unlock for robots, exosuits , and automation 🦾

Today we’re sharing a milestone toward that vision:
New in Science Robotics — Electrofluidic Fiber Muscles (EFM), a new class of soft, electrically driven artificial muscles. https://lnkd.in/deZejxxA

A collaboration between my group at the RoboPhysicsLab at Politecnico di Bari (Italy) and the Tangible Media Group at MIT Media Lab (US) .
We developed sub-2 mm fibers that deliver 50 W/kg (comparable to human muscle) and contract 20% in 0.3 s. They use fluids driven by electric fields and operate silently — with no external pumps or reservoirs. By integrating electrohydrodynamic (EHD) fiber pumps into a sealed, untethered system, we address long-standing challenges in portability and scalability in soft robotics.

🔬 Key insight: coupling fluidic actuators with an integrated pump requires pre-pressurizing the system at an optimal bias pressure.
We developed a model that predicts this pressure and guides how to arrange pumps and actuators in modular parallel–series configurations to scale force and speed.

Applications span soft robotics, wearable assistive devices, and dexterous systems — with modular scaling up and actuation embedded directly into robots bodies or textiles.

Huge thanks to a fabulous team!
Lead author: Ozgun Kilic Afsar, PhD student at MIT
PI: Vito Cacucciolo, Prof. at Politecnico di Bari
Team: Gabriele Pupillo, Gennaro Vitucci (Politecnico di Bari), Wedyan Babatain, Hiroshi Ishii (Massachusetts Institute of Technology)
