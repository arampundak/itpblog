Star Wars vs. Star Trek

Are We picturing a Dystopia or Utopia with Humanoid Robots?

Take a moment to picture the USS Cerritos and the X-wing.
Notice the stark contrast in their designs?

Star Wars, with its dystopian flair, leans heavily on triangles, sharp edges, broken lines, and muted, dirty hues.

Meanwhile, Star Trek’s utopian vision showcases curves, soft edges, symmetrical forms, smooth lines, and light, pristine panels.

This isn’t just a stylistic coincidence—it’s a deliberate choice to nudge our imagination toward either a dystopian or utopian future.

The same principle applies to humanoid robot designs. Do we want our robots to signal a dystopian future? Or perhaps, the opposite? Regardless of your answer, the current trend in humanoid robot design seems to point toward a utopian vision.

These robots are not built to look like weapons. Instead, they resemble sleek consumer electronics or even medical devices. Think smooth, rounded corners, bright, vibrant shades of grey, clean, defined LEDs, and continuous, flowing lines across their panels.
Their bodies are symmetrical, and their heads are designed with a soft, humane shape.
Even behind the mysterious dark visor of their heads, you might catch a playful smile now and then.

These thoughtful design choices make these robots more appealing—welcoming even. We’re inclined to imagine them alongside us, in our homes or at work.

I’m not here to weigh the pros and cons. I simply enjoy observing the evolution of their design and wondering how it will change.

But what about you? Do these robots evoke the rugged dystopia of Star Wars or the hopeful utopia of Star Trek?
