What happens to human connection when a robot takes sides? 🤖💬

We’re excited to share our recent paper, “The Impact of a Robot’s Agreement (or Disagreement) on Human–Human Interpersonal Closeness in a Two-Person Decision-Making Task”, has now been published in Computers in Human Behavior! 🎉

This work is based on a collaboration between Reichman University’s milab - Media Innovation Lab and the University of Chicago Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) Lab, led by Dr. Sarah Sebo.

Authored by Ting-Han (Timmy) L., Yuval Rubin Kopelman, Madeline Busse, Sarah Sebo, and Dr. Hadas Erel, the paper explores how feedback from humanoid and non-humanoid robots can influence the closeness between two people.

👥 In the study, pairs of participants completed a joint decision-making task with a robot that either agreed or disagreed with their answers and treated them equally or unequally. Two parallel experiments were conducted, one with Nao, a humanoid robot capable of speech and gestures, and another with Kip, a simple non-humanoid robot communicating only through movement.

💡 Findings show that the robot design and it's feedback shaped the quality of human–human relationships, emphasizing the responsibility of designers to consider how robots may influence social dynamics between humans.

A heartfelt thank you to Noa Kirtchuk, Mai Efrat, Hila Zohar, Andrey Grishko, Nevo Heimann Saadon, Esha Mujumdar, and Eden Lulu for their incredible support!
