## Full text

I had a conversation recently with a designer who had applied to over 100 roles and hadn’t landed a single interview.

They weren’t lacking experience. Their portfolio was actually very full — internships, school projects, different types of work — all bundled together and sent to every role they applied to.

The same portfolio went out whether the role was in advertising, social media, or something more specialized. From their side, it felt thorough. From a hiring manager’s side, it likely made it hard to quickly understand where this person was actually a fit.

The main issue wasn’t the quality of the work.
 It was relevance and clarity.

What this usually leads to is wasted effort. People spend weeks rewriting resumes, redesigning portfolios, or applying to more roles — without knowing whether any of that is addressing the real problem.

In this case, the most valuable change wasn’t “do more.”
It was knowing what to stop doing, what to ignore for now, and what actually needed adjustment.

That’s exactly what I do in my job search audit. It’s a paid diagnostic where I assess someone’s portfolio, positioning, and job search approach to identify where your job search funnel is breaking down — and what to change first so time and effort aren’t wasted fixing the wrong things.

I’ve added a link to the job search audit in the comments for anyone who wants help figuring out what to adjust first.
