## Full text

I recently experienced autonomous delivery robots in a city where they're already part of everyday life, and I was fascinated... not just by the technology, but by how people interacted with them.

Everyone seemed to care about them. People stepped aside, helped them when they got stuck, and there are already countless memes showing them delivering through snow, heavy rain, or even AI-generated videos imagining them cleaning cities or doing funny tasks.

It made me realize that one of the hardest design challenges isn't only making the robot work, it's deciding how it should make people feel.

My girlfriend's mother made me laugh when she said:

"I don't use them because they're too cute. I feel bad making them work."

It's funny, but it also highlights a real design trade-off. A friendly appearance builds trust and empathy, yet it can also create emotional resistance to using the product.

As engineers and designers, we spend countless hours optimizing performance, reliability, and manufacturability. But sometimes, a design choice that seems purely aesthetic can completely change how people behave.
