## Full text

Google DeepMind just dropped Gemini Robotics 2.

The physical AI model comes in three flavors: the standard Gemini Robotics 2 VLA (vision language action mod); Gemini Robotics ER 2, a VLM (vision language model) for embodied reasoning/human to robot communication; and Gemini Robotics On-Device 2, an edge-based model. All three are available in early-access form.

A post by DeepMind's VP and Head of Robotics, Carolina Parada, highlights the models' use on humanoid and other bi-manual embodiments. Apollo 2, the humanoid Apptronik revealed in the lead up to June's Automate Show, takes center stage in the post. The company has been testing the models at Robot Park, a massive data collection facility in Austin, TX.

According to humanoid firm "This software allows Apptronik's Apollo 2 to do full-body autonomous functions, showing advanced reasoning in real time."

This appears to also be where embodied reasoning and natural language communication come in. Per Google, "For example, when controlling Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid robot, we can ask it to 'put the watering can into the green bin in the bottom shelf.' Apollo processes the instruction, walks to the table, and picks up the watering can, takes a few steps to the shelves, and places it precisely in its destination."

Parada notes that full-body movement still has a way to go, with regard to speed.

ER 2 also allows the systems to execute complex, multi-step tasks, correcting themselves and carrying on, should they get stuck somewhere in the middle.

The teams have been using Wave hands from Sharpa for highly dexterous tasks, including knot tying and bag sealing. The system also operates with the two-fingered Franka Duo system.

Also present is multi-robot communication. This is a big potential unlock for industrial settings, allowing different embodiments to communicate and coordinate in uniform workflows.

ASIMOV-Agentic is a new addition on the safety front. DeepMind refers to it as a "benchmark for agentic safety orchestration and uncertainty resolution." This allows the robot to reject commands involving potentially unsafe tool usage and -- if it's not certain whether it can complete a given task -- call for human assistance.
