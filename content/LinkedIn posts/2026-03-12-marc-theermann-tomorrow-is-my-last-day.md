## Full text

Tomorrow is my last day at Boston Dynamics.
Five years ago, Rob gave me the opportunity of a lifetime when he invited me to join his executive team to help commercialize the company's groundbreaking inventions. 

Alongside a remarkable team, I developed a world-class go-to-market organization that now employs nearly 300 people, capable of predicting and generating hundreds of millions in revenue while deploying and servicing robots globally.

When I started at BD, autonomous, legged robots were just a twinkle in the eye of even the brightest investors, and commercial humanoids seemed like pure science fiction. 

Now that these ideas are rapidly becoming a reality for industrial applications, I find myself envisioning the possibilities for consumer & entertainment robots. For decades, I have been dreaming about building cute companion robots. It was the original reason I left my AdTech career at Google to join Boston Dynamics. 

It feels like the stars are finally aligning to make magical consumer robots possible: 

The costs of actuators, sensors, and chips are significantly decreasing
Large language models can facilitate powerful, enduring relationships
Robot foundational models are getting dramatically better
AI provides semantic understanding of unchartered environments 

The next paradigm shift in robotics will be the introduction of embodied, advanced cognitive systems that can perceive and model the world around us, maintain situational awareness and create emotional relationships with humans.

Now envision enchanting animatronics on top of existing robots, and completing the software stack with a character engine and an emotional software wrapper to create deep engagement. I am ready to pull the string on that thread and see where it takes me. 

Very soon characters will step off the screen, and into our living rooms, and humans will live, learn and laugh with robots. 

Dynamic Creatures and the illusion of life are around the corner, and that is what I intend to chase next. 

It has been a fascinating journey, and the honor of my life to serve with an exceptional executive team, my fantastic go-to-market team, our amazing clients, and our brilliant engineers. I am especially grateful for the unwavering support and love that poured in over the last few days. 

Thank you all so very much, and may the force be with you. 

Marc

PS: My biggest regret at BD is that I did not get a chance to meet my favorite director Jon Favreau. I had dreamed of showing him our robots, and perhaps working on a fun SW collaboration. Perhaps at the next place ;)
