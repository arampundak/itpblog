## Full text

I have to weigh into these discussions. Both of these robots are large humanoids that are mostly teleoperated. On the left you can see Steve, a robot I created 12 years ago. On the right the robot called NEO, by 1X.

Yesterday 1X announced an update on their NEO humanoid robot. Although devoid of any kind of friendly face or empathic anker points, the hardware platform looks very impressive. The robot operates "partly autonomous", according to 1X, but lets be honest: It is teleoperated by somebody in the next room. They didn't even bother to use an LLM, like Steve. The voice is just the operator speaking in a microphone.

Apart from the mobility, it is exactly the same concept as Steve. 
Steve is mostly autonomous, but needs our help moving around, so it can stay very close to people, without hurting them. The operator could see and hear everything Steve could perceive from another location.
Steve also had his 11th anniversary last month, and he has his next public job planned next month. Steve was years ahead of his time, and he is still going strong. By the looks of it, many humanoid robot companies are still trying to catch up on the empathic side of the humanoids story. In the end, clients will decide which robot they feel most comfortable with.
