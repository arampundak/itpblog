## Full text

Some cool lessons by Rui Xu in this article by The Robot Report:

https://lnkd.in/gSj-Cyy4

Some comments to his points:
1) Yes. Software only eats hardware if you _really_ understand the hardware, too. And its constraints. However, I'm not sure if end stops are a good solution in this concrete case. If you hit an end-stop full-speed, things will brake... the energy has to go somewhere. Yes, hopefully it deforms metal and not a human, but the overall safety concept of such machines is complex.

2) Yes. Where are all these hoverboards, though? I also like the compression-analogy. The same applies to human language: You have complex constructs in your mind, need to convey them with low-bandwidth, analog language, and the listener has to de-compress it, and you have to hope that they form the same construct that you have in your mind.

3) Yes. There is a huge team(s) at maxon taking care of supply chains, quality, logistics. It's ba-na-nas what's needed to ship hardware on time, on quality!

4) The question is: Where do you draw the "commodity-barrier"? Some subsystems are becoming a commodity, I think. The magic is in the overall integration and smart customization (where possible). The whole hardware robotics ecosystem has come a huge way in the last decade (think compute, sensors, batteries, actuators, PCB-manufacturers, 3D-printing, injection molding, fast prototyping etc. etc.)

5) I think R&D is just one puzzle-piece. The overall _product_ has to work. The magic is defining this _product_ as a sufficiently simple use-case, without blowing the complexity out of the water such that R&D can never converge. There's an important difference between a system and a product.

6) Yes. Very important company culture topic! There is a different between "movement" and "progress". A regular, well-defined, company-wide execution rhythm is also important. Focus, execute, learn, redefine, repeat.

"I still believe in embodied AI. I just believe it deserves hardware that's as seriously engineered as the software controlling it." Yes. The magic is in the combination. But it's very hard magic...

Onwards!
