## Full text

Sunday, a home robot startup, has raised $165M.


But the more interesting part is the bet behind it.

Let me explain.

Instead of training robots mainly through other robots doing tasks - autonomously or teleoperated, they are letting humans do everyday tasks naturally in real environments, and capture that behavior using tools that mirror how a robot would move later, like a glove that acts like a robotic hand.

The core insight is this. 

Robotics doesn’t struggle because robots are “not smart enough.” It struggles because real-world data is expensive, slow, and painfully repetitive to collect at scale.

Homes are the hardest environment you can think of. 

Nothing is standard

- a drawer in one house is different from another 
- a cup is placed differently every time 

And robots need to see that chaos.

So the shift here is subtle but important.

Instead of scaling robots to gather data, they scale humans with lightweight tools and let reality become the dataset.

From a builder’s POV, this is huge. 

If this works, robotics stops being just about building better machines.
It becomes about designing better ways to capture human behavior so machines can actually learn from the real world.

And that changes the center of gravity of the entire field.
