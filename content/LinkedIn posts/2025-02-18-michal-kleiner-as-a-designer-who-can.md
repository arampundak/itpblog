## Full text

As a designer who can spend hours fine tuning lighting and refining materials in rendering software, I initially worried that using AI would turn me into a machine, simply feeding prompts into a model. After all, these technical skills have shaped how my fellow designers and I understand 3D, how lighting behaves, what feels aesthetically right, and how to bring our visions to life.
There has been plenty of discussion about AI as a tool alongside existing design software, and at Manyone, we are actively integrating it into our workflow.

AI does not replace our current tools, it enhances them.
It accelerates brainstorming, allows us to quickly explore compositions and design environments, and seamlessly feeds back into our traditional processes.

In the video, you can see how I merge a KeyShot rendering, a Midjourney generated environment, and a light animation from Runway.
By combining our expertise with AI’s capabilities, we broaden our creative possibilities and gain a wider range of options.

Often, AI tools serve as a starting point, whether for designing an initial sketch or as a brainstorming aid, helping us refine ideas collaboratively with our clients and push creative boundaries. It allows us to step into different roles - 3D modelers, creative directors, animators - for a moment, giving us the freedom to refine our vision before transitioning into other software.

Like it or not, AI is not magic. But it can add a spark of creativity to every design process, expanding our capabilities and opening doors to new creative territories.
