Robotics Has a Massive Talent Shortage Problem

A lot of people think robotics is overcrowded and breaking in is extremely hard. Given the current job market and the noise around tech, that belief makes sense.

But from what I’ve seen in industry, the reality is often very different. Many competitive robotics roles stay open for months, or never get filled with the right candidate. This happens despite strong academic programs and more students entering the field every year. The issue isn’t lack of interest or opportunity, it’s lack of alignment with what industry actually needs.
The hardest candidates to find tend to combine strong fundamentals, depth in at least one domain, the ability to take ideas from algorithms to hardware, and experience working in large collaborative software systems. That combination is surprisingly rare.

1. Universities optimize for publications, not industry readiness
Graduate programs focus on research output. Students build deep expertise, but often lack exposure to large integrated systems. Work is usually split into isolated projects, which limits system-level intuition. In robotics, building real systems is part of the science, and without that, many students face a skill gap when moving to industry.

2. Companies aren’t clear about role scope
Job descriptions often blur multiple specializations together. This makes it hard for candidates to know how they’ll be evaluated and how to prepare, and it makes hiring inefficient on the company side.

3. Candidates lack clear signals on where they stand
Many early-career roboticists don’t know how “ready” they are for different roles or what to prioritize. This question often shows up late, under pressure, when graduation is near. In short, the entire process is like shooting in the dark and hoping for the best. 

I’m curious:

If you’re early in your career, what feels most unclear about preparing for industry?
 If you’ve hired in robotics, where do candidates most often fall short?
 If you’ve made the transition, what helped you bridge the gap?

Would love to hear different perspectives in the comments.
