## Full text

If you’re in robotics, you’ve probably heard some of these debates.

Every time I go into a meeting with robotics companies, one of my little brain games is to guess where people land on a few recurring "debates". Over the years, I’ve noticed the same opposing opinions coming back again and again. Some people hold them very rigidly, others insist it’s always a spectrum. Either way, the patterns are there.

Think of these as robotics mindset axes, similar to an MBTI test.
For each axis, pick the side that fits you best and stack the letters. You might end up as something like A S U G W R E.

1. Application vs Technology
A:
Any robot should be developed for a clear, well-defined use case.
T:
Applications will come later. The priority is building strong, general-purpose technology.

2. Software vs Hardware
S:
Modern robots are mostly about software and AI.
H:
Hardware still has a long way to go. Without it, software does not matter much.

3. Fun vs Function (Utility)
F:
Humanoids mainly make sense for entertainment, social interaction, and research.
U:
The real reason to invest in humanoids is to make them do useful, functional work, including industrial tasks.

4. Specialized vs Generalist
S:
One robot, one job. Single-application robots are the only realistic path.
G:
Single-application robots are short-sighted. We need robots that can do many things.

5. Legs vs Wheels
L:
If we say humanoid, we mean biped. Legs are essential.
W:
Bipeds are overrated. A wheeled base with a humanoid upper body is far more practical.

6. Cost Optimism vs Realism
O:
In a few years, we will see millions of humanoids priced lower than a car.
R:
There is no realistic path for humanoid robots to become cheap anytime soon.

7. Optimization vs Exploration
P:
We should stop inventing new platforms and focus on perfecting existing ones.
E:
There is no perfect platform yet. New form factors and architectures still need to be explored.

So… what’s your combo?
