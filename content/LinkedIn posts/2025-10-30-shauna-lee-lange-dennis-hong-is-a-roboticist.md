## Full text

Dennis Hong is a #roboticist and #mechanicalengineer who is also the founder of the Robotics & Mechanisms Laboratory (RoMeLa) at UCLA, where research explores robot locomotion, manipulation, novel mechanisms and human-robot interaction. 

In 2022, among his more playful yet conceptually rich projects, was a robot with a balloon body, known as the “balloon-bodied” robot or balloon-powered robot, created to use #buoyancy to aid stability and reduce the risk of falls in legged #machines. An #artexhibition known as "Lightness of Submission" aligned with Hong’s balloon-bodied robot concept: a helium-filled mylar #balloon torso combined with articulated legs, which enabled floating and walking. The balloon allowed a robot to interact safely with humans or uneven terrain. 

What makes this piece interesting for the intersection of #artandtechnology is how it blends rigorous engineering and physical dynamics (stability, locomotion, robot design) with expressive, performative qualities (floating, walking, being fragile yet mobile). The balloon body evokes notions of lightness, impermanence, buoyancy and surprise, which contrast with the typically heavy, rigid, metallic image of robots. 

It invites viewers to reconsider what a #robot can be and how form and intention influence perception. In an exhibition or art-context, this work challenges assumptions about robotics as purely functional or industrial. It becomes poetic: the robot submission to buoyant force, the gentle walk on twig-like legs, the visual metaphor of a machine floating and moving. It invites reflection on presence, motion, levity, human-machine interaction, and the boundary between built mechanism and artistic gesture.

This year (2025), Science World featured an article titled “Robot Designer” that profiled Hong and his work at the Laboratory, detailing current robot projects and his vision for household and #assistiverobotics. Hong announced in May that his team would present 6 live demonstrations at the 2025 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA) under the hashtag #ICRA2025.
