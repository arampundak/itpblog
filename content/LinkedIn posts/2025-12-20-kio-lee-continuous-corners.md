Continuous Corners

27 years ago, I learned from a senior designer why the angled bezels of Sony's Trinitron TVs looked more stable and premium than those of other brands. Instead of simply rounding the corners when connecting straight lines at 90-degree angles, Sony adopted this technique. Many products today use this technique, and I believe Apple explicitly uses it as well.

The actual process wasn't that difficult, requiring only a bit of labor and observation. All I had to do was create a template and adjust its size. It wasn't perfect, but it was better than starting from scratch. Recently, my designers were interested in this technique, so I explained it to them.

This technique can be applied not only to physical product design but also to on-screen icon design. Using rounded rectangles when applying masking to icons can create a premium feel by applying the principles described above. Apple has used this technique successfully since its inception, but Android's early "Material Design" UI system didn't use it, which made it look somewhat clunky. It's been around for quite some time, but I'm not sure how it's doing these days.

Anyway, I think it's really cool that designers pay attention to and study these small details that might not be immediately noticeable but have a big impact. I hope more designers will do this in the future.
