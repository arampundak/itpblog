
### 1. Terminal, Shell, and OS (Conceptual Clarification)

- **Shell** = the original _user interface_ of Unix-based operating systems (pre-GUI).
    
    - Historical variants: sh (Bourne shell), csh, ksh, bash, zsh.
        
- **Terminal** = a _program_ that runs the shell.
    
    - Historically: a physical terminal.
        
    - Today: macOS Terminal.app, iTerm, etc.
        
- Key distinction:
    
    - **Shell = UI logic**
        
    - **Terminal = container that runs the shell**
        

This clarified a foundational misunderstanding about command line vs terminal vs OS.

### 2. Biggest Student Misunderstanding in the Course

- Students often assume the course is about producing a **big final “masterpiece.”**
    
- Tom emphasizes:
    
    - The course is about **learning skills, context, and fundamentals**
        
    - Assignments are **exercises**, not expressive or portfolio-driven artifacts
        
- Success = learning _how_ to think about connected systems, not building something flashy.
    

---

### 3. Core Skills the Course Is Teaching

- Basics of **networking and connected devices**
    
- Understanding **client–server communication**
    
- Introduction to **communication protocols**
    
- Knowing _where to start_ when designing systems that connect devices
    
- Focus is on **foundational literacy**, not mastery

### 4. Devices ≠ Objects (Important Reframe)

- Tom corrected a key conceptual drift:
    
    - The course is **not about turning data into physical objects**
        
    - It’s about **devices we interact with**
        
- Devices may represent data via:
    
    - screens
        
    - lights
        
    - sound
        
- Physicalizing data into objects is **usually not recommended** by default.
    
- This distinction is why he renamed the course from _Networkjects_ → _Connected Devices_.

### 5. What Makes a “Good” Project in This Class

- Tom reframed “good projects” as **good learning trajectories**
    
- Example of a strong project:
    
    - Long-term sensing of something _real_ in the student’s environment
        
    - Minimal intervention, reliable sensing
        
    - Letting patterns emerge over time
        
- Example: sensing restroom traffic via directional sensing (vector, not just distance)
    
- Value comes from:
    
    - Observing slow changes
        
    - Discovering patterns we normally overlook

### 6. Surveillance, Behavior Change, and Ethics

- Acknowledged that **being sensed can change behavior**
    
- Core ethical question:
    
    > What is the _minimum amount of information_ needed to answer a question?
    
- Key distinctions:
    
    - Identifying _activity_ vs identifying _people_
        
    - Sensor design and physical appearance affect whether people _feel watched_
        
- Encouraged **low-stakes sensing projects** first
    
    - Learn the _how_ before tackling the _why_
        

---

### 7. Responsible Connected Devices (Tom’s View)

Responsibility =

- Minimizing negative impact on people and environments
    
- Gathering _only_ the data needed
    
- Avoiding behavior changes that make things worse
    

He rejects blanket “tech is bad” narratives:

- Side effects are inevitable
    
- Tech is neither inherently good nor bad
    
- Hyper-critical academic positions often claim neutrality but aren’t
    

---

### 8. Sensors That Change Behavior for the Better

Examples:

- Light sensors used correctly to improve environmental design
    
- Sensors in assistive and prosthetic technologies that adapt to users
    
- Good design _can_ change behavior positively

### 9. Robotics, Eyes, and Personification

- Tom is skeptical of:
    
    - Adding “eyes” to devices without clear purpose
        
    - Googly-eye aesthetics in tech
        
- Strong concern about:
    
    - Robotics trends that **devalue human labor**
        
    - Deploying robots without social consideration
        
- He is more open to:
    
    - Eyes on humanoid robots _only_ when they communicate orientation/attention
        
    - Robotics in **geriatric and healthcare contexts**, where demographic needs are real
        

---

### 10. Intent, Trust, and AI Alignment

- Referenced **The Alignment Problem**
    
    - Misalignment between:
        
        - AI designers’ intent
            
        - AI systems’ behavior
            
        - Users’ expectations
            
- Core point:
    
    - You can often assume shared intent among humans
        
    - You _cannot_ assume intent from machines—even if they have “eyes”
        
- This makes personification potentially misleading rather than reassuring
    

---

### 11. Caution Around Big Tech Waves

- Tom advocates for **slowing down** large technological deployments
    
- Referenced **The Box**
    
    - Container shipping reshaped cities, labor, and culture
        
    - Unintended consequences can be both destructive and generative
        
- Lesson:
    
    - Technological “misfortunes” sometimes enable new cultural futures
        
    - But we shouldn’t rush tech adoption just to see what happens

### 12. Personal Philosophy (Tone + Values)

- Skeptical of over-personifying machines
    
- Concerned that fascination with robots distracts from humans
    
- Sees parallels between:
    
    - Web boom → crash
        
    - AI hype → current moment
        
- Believes responsibility means:
    
    - Seeing both advantages _and_ costs
        
    - Resisting speed driven purely by capital
