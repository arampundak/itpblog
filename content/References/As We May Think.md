Vannevar Bush's **"[As We May Think](https://www.google.com/search?q=as+we+may+think&kgmid=/m/0l80&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwiYpeOW8MyTAxXJk4kEHeqYA5IQ3egRegQIAhAC),"** first published in [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/1945/07/as-we-may-think/303881/) in July 1945, is one of the most influential essays in the history of information science and computing. Written as World War II was ending, Bush challenged scientists to shift their focus from destruction to the task of making the massive store of human knowledge more accessible.

---

**The Problem: Information Overload**
Bush observed that while the amount of research was growing exponentially, the methods for finding and using it remained "generations old". He warned that scientists were being "bogged down" by specialization and an "information explosion" they could no longer navigate.

**The Vision: The Memex**
The centerpiece of the essay is the **Memex** (a portmanteau of "memory" and "index"). Bush envisioned it as a futuristic, mechanized desk for personal use that would serve as an "intimate supplement" to human memory.
