Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative (CITI) human subjects training.
Provides essential education on ethical principles, human subject protections, and regulatory compliance for researchers.

- Go to the CITI Program and click **Register**. [[1](https://research.fiu.edu/irb/training-requirements/)]
- Search for and select your specific **Institution/Organization** affiliation. [[1](https://research.fiu.edu/irb/training-requirements/)]
- Complete your profile details and choose the required **Human Subjects Research (HSR)** learner group or course track. [[1](https://research.fiu.edu/irb/training-requirements/)]
- Finish all required modules and save or download your **Completion Certificate** for your IRB submission. [[1](https://www.csusm.edu/research/compliance/irb/training.html)]

**How the IRB works and whom to talk to**

NYU has a central IRB/Human Research Protection Program office. Its general address is [ask.humansubjects@nyu.edu](mailto:ask.humansubjects@nyu.edu), and the director is Scott Fisher. The office helps students prepare applications and clarifies what is required.

The process:

1. **Talk to a faculty sponsor first.** NYU requires one when a student is the PI. That would be an ITP faculty member, for example your advisor or the instructor of the class this project is for. They help decide whether you need to submit at all, and they sign off on the application.
2. **Complete CITI training.** This is the online course, a few hours, and everyone on the application needs it before review.
3. **Submit a protocol in Cayuse.** It describes what you’ll do, who the participants are, your recruitment message, your consent form, and how you’ll store recordings.
4. **Wait for the IRB’s decision** (exempt, expedited, or full review). Only after approval can you recruit or record anyone.
