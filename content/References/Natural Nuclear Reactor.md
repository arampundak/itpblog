The world's only known natural nuclear fission reactors are located at ==Oklo in Gabon, Western Africa==. Formed roughly 1.7 to 2 billion years ago, these 17 (or 16, depending on the source) sites in the Franceville basin operated as self-sustaining nuclear reactors for hundreds of thousands of years, utilizing natural groundwater as a neutron moderator. 

**Key Details About the Oklo Natural Reactor**

- **Discovery:** The site was discovered in 1972 by French physicists who noticed an anomaly in the ratio of uranium isotopes in ore, indicating that had been "burned".
- **Mechanism:** High-grade uranium deposits, combined with groundwater acting as a moderator, allowed for a sustained, natural, self-regulating chain reaction.
- **Operation:** The reactors ran for roughly 150,000 to 200,000 years, producing about 100 kW of power.
- **Why It Can't Happen Now:** At the time of formation, the concentration of the fissile isotope was higher (about 3%) than it is today (0.72%), which is too low to sustain a natural reaction without artificial enrichment.
- **Environmental Impact:** Despite 2 billion years, the nuclear waste products (fission products) did not migrate far from the original site, providing valuable data for modern geological disposal of nuclear waste.
