The **Heider-Simmel Illusion** is ==a psychological effect where people instinctively assign human emotions, personalities, and social narratives to simple, moving geometric shapes==

The **Heider-Simmel Illusion** is a landmark psychological phenomenon originating from a **1944 experiment** by Fritz Heider and Marianne Simmel. It showcases the powerful, involuntary human tendency to **attribute social intent, emotions, and personality traits to inanimate objects** based strictly on their movement. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Simmel), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g7kLjozLiyM), [3](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DFn-jXDyzPj/?hl=en)]

All papers about robotic motion uses this as an example and reference.
