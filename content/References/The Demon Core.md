The Demon Core was a 6.2 kg (13.7 lb) plutonium-gallium sphere intended for a third [U.S. atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon_core) during WWII. After Japan's surrender, it was used at Los Alamos for critical experiments, causing fatal radiation accidents in 1945 and 1946 that killed Harry Daghlian and [Louis Slotin](https://www.newyorker.com/tech/annals-of-technology/demon-core-the-strange-death-of-louis-slotin). The core was later melted down and recycled. 

**Key Details About the Demon Core:**

- **Purpose:** Originally designed as the fissile core for a third "Fat Man" type nuclear bomb.
- **Accidents:**
**1945:** Physicist Harry Daghlian dropped a tungsten carbide brick on the core, causing a supercritical reaction; he died 25 days later
**1946:** Physicist Louis Slotin was conducting a, dangerous, "tickling the dragon's tail" experiment using a screwdriver to lower a beryllium reflector dome. The screwdriver slipped, causing a blue flash of light and a massive radiation burst, killing Slotin nine days later.

- **Composition:** A solid 3.5-inch sphere of plutonium-gallium alloy.

- **Final Fate:** Following the 1946 accident, the "demon" nickname solidified, and the core was later melted down and repurposed for future nuclear research, ending its existence as a single, dangerous unit.
