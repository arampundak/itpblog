When writing a mind map it is good to let out our most initial thoughts, the first layer might be the most superficial one, we have to get it out of the system in order for fresh and novel ideas to have room.

>A mind map is a great tool for visually organizing information and ideas. It allows you to take your stream of consciousness and give it structure, helping you analyze and synthesize new ideas. 
>The organizational structure of a mind map is simple and intuitive: the main idea is placed in the middle and additional sub-topics and concepts branch out from the center. More information can continue to branch from the sub-topics and so on.
> _Different projects might require distinct formats of mind maps.  
> Don't be afraid to change and adapt._

Arjun the king says:
- While developing a mind map always ask "why?": why am I writing what i'm writing, is it important / bad / good
- At the end of the process leave with questions to think about before coming back to the mind map work
- Ask yourself - What do I want to explore?
- Start nodes with a question
- How to get to the next node - ask "for what?"
- Think of roots that will affect your process of mind mapping
- Always have a field for what has been done before, and how can I build upon this
