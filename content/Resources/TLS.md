Transport Layer Security (TLS) is ==the standard cryptographic protocol designed to provide secure, encrypted communications over computer networks like the internet==. As the successor to Secure Sockets Layer (SSL), it ensures data privacy, integrity, and authenticity, most commonly seen as HTTPS in web browsers.

- **Security Functions:** TLS provides encryption (hiding data), authentication (verifying parties), and integrity (ensuring data hasn't been tampered with).
- **The Handshake:** Before data transmission, a "TLS handshake" occurs, allowing the server and client to authenticate each other and establish encryption keys.
- **Common Usage:** Primarily used in HTTPS, but also secures email, instant messaging, and voice-over-IP.
- **TLS Certificates:** Websites require a TLS/SSL certificate from a certificate authority to verify their identity and enable encrypted sessions.
