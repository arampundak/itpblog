Where Good Ideas Come From: The Natural History of Innovation
Book by Steven Johnson
Core Premise
- **The "Lone Genius" Myth:** Johnson argues that breakthrough ideas rarely belong to isolated inventors.
- **Ecosystems of Creativity:** Ideas grow best in rich, connected environments—much like coral reefs or busy cities.
- **Recombination:** Most breakthroughs mix existing ideas in new ways.
Johnson identifies seven recurring conditions that drive creative breakthroughs across history:
- **The Adjacent Possible:** Environments contain a specific set of neighboring ideas that are ready to be explored next.
- **Liquid Networks:** Unstructured, open spaces allow incomplete thoughts to collide and evolve.
- **The Slow Hunch:** Important ideas take years to incubate and mature in the back of the mind.
- **Serendipity:** Happy accidents and unexpected connections spark new directions.
- **Error:** Mistakes force people to look at problems through a fresh lens.
- **Exaptation:** Tools or traits are repurposed for entirely new uses.
- **Platforms:** Basic foundational layers emerge, allowing others to build newer inventions on top of them.
