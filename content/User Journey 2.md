## Still needing thought:

**Pure Socratic questioning fails beginners.** There’s well-established evidence that minimal guidance works poorly for novices (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006, “Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work”). I added a rule: after two unanswered questions, offer options with trade-offs and let the student choose. Without that rule, Ari becomes the annoying tutor who won’t answer.

**Your procedure/design line is blurry.** Laser power and speed settings are both procedure and design. A cleaner principle is consequence and reversibility:

- Irreversible or unsafe choices get direct answers.
- Reversible choices that a cheap test cut can teach go back to the student.


**A failed cut is troubleshooting, not a design decision.** “Didn’t cut all the way” usually comes down to focus, power, or speed. That’s procedure. File 2 has Ari ask what the student thinks happened, then confirm directly. “What would you change?” fits better when the decision is about the design itself.

## The user journey:

1. Staff are busy with someone else. Ari is on its dock, eye closed, antennas up. A passer-by walks on, and Ari stays quiet
2. A student says the wake phrase.
3. Ari: “How can I help?”
4. Student "I want help with making: a sundial for the time class"
5. Ari asks a couple of questions about the project. File ready? Material? Have you used the laser machine before?
6. “Pick me up, let’s go to the laser.” “Are we there yet?”
7. “I can guide you better if I can see. Open my visor?” Ari’s view is framed on the machine bed and turns around to the user.
8. Procedure as dialogue. Ari notices someone waiting: “Mind if they listen in?” The student agrees and Ari invites the other person.
9. A bystander leans into view, and Ari narrows its view back to the bed.
10. Test cut. Didn't cut all the way...
11. Student shows it to Ari
12. Ari turns to the student and quietly asks what they’d change.
13. Staff come over for the final check, and Ari defers.
14. “Done? Want me to send you a summary? You can close my visor”. “I need your help to go back to my spot.”
15. The person who listened starts their own session: “I heard the how to focus part.” Their session is shorter.
