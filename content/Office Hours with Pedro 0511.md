**What he’s telling you**

These are mostly one point: right now the project rests on your own assumptions, and he wants evidence. Here’s how the notes group:

1. **Define success in a way you can measure.** He gave you the measure: fewer questions to shop staff, and students finishing tasks on their own. That should be the main claim of the project and of the HRI paper. Without it, Ari is a nice character with nothing to show for it.
2. **Test a matrix, not one finished robot.** “Prototype with matrix” and “personalities to check different features” go together. Pick 2–3 variables, for example tone (Socratic vs. direct), when Ari speaks (only when asked vs. offering help), and camera (on vs. off). Test the combinations instead of polishing one version.
3. **Get past n=1.** You’re one ITP student designing for ITP students in a shop you know well. Talk to staff and users in other shops: the Tandon makerspace, IDM, maybe a public makerspace. Ask what questions people actually bring to staff. That tests whether the problem exists outside your own experience.
4. **Justify every design decision.** Why the shop, why this form factor, why moving it to the machine, why a camera. The form-factor question is still open (boxy vs. round), and Pedro has already pushed back on it. You need evidence or a test result for it, not taste.
5. **Playtest it rough, with people who know nothing about it.** Don’t wait for the wood shell. If you explain it before the test, the test is useless.
