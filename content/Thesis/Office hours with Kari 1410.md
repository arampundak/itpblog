I’m moving on several fronts at once. That might suit me better than a linear path, because whichever thread becomes clear first pulls the others along.

**HRI 2027 Student Design Competition.** Following Kari’s earlier suggestion, I’m applying. I have five weeks and I’m roughly 20% in. At first I worried my project only loosely fit the brief. I now care less about that and believe more in the work.

**Public vs. semi-public.** The ITP shop is semi-public. ==Kari’s advice was to prove the “public” claim by taking the robot out of the place where I develop it.== That could be the Tandem makerspace, which serves all of NYU, or another NYC makerspace. Her example was a sociologist in her 3D printing training who was reproducing Neolithic tools. Open makerspaces are full of non-experts like that.

**Broaden the machine story.** The laser cutter is still my test case, but ==most public makerspaces, libraries and classrooms have 3D printers== or Glowforge-style desktop lasers, not CO2 lasers like ours. ==The paper should speak to those machines.==

==**Choosing a real complication.**== The brief requires me to tackle at least one real complication. My first idea was “a bystander overhears a tutoring session.” Kari pointed out that this isn’t a complication, because nobody expects privacy in a shop. Uninvited interaction more likely means bad actors, or the robot interrupting someone. ==My strongest interest is surveillance and consent.== I’m planning a physical visor that a user has to move to let the robot see. Kari asked whether its ears turn off too. Options:
- a wake word
- a directional mic so the robot only hears whoever it is facing
Whether a ==directional mic can isolate one voice in a noisy shop== is a question for an audio expert.

**Safety systems.** Kari suggested ==comparing the robot to existing makerspace safety systems.== Some spaces use card access tied to completed training. Others, like ours, rely on a social contract. The question is whether the robot sits somewhere between the two.
==card access <--> social contract==

**Stretch myself.** Form-making is in my comfort zone. The ==research-by-making I’ve been avoiding is coding the robot and actually talking to it.== I’m pairing with Aditya on the system architecture. My assignment before next week is to spend real time talking to the robot and to reflect on why that feels uncomfortable.

**IRB.** The competition doesn’t require one. If I pursue a PhD later, I can run a proper study on the same platform then.

**The bigger thesis.** I tried to articulate the question behind all this. My claim is that AI’s harm depends on interaction design, not on the technology itself. Kari pushed me to bound that claim to social harm. Economic, ecological and cognitive harms, and the ethics of scraping training data, are real but different problems. Without that boundary, the argument gets diluted.

==**Don’t focus yet.**== We’re in week 6 of 28. Kari’s advice was explicitly not to narrow down. Locking in now probably means leaving good ideas unexplored. If I fall in love with the shop robot, the path is depth: more spaces, more tools, more of the unsolved complications. If I fall out of love with it, I move on. ==Either outcome is fine.==
