Summarize by LLM:

The study measured real-world light exposure at eye level in factory and office spaces over eight months using miniature spectrometer sensors. It found that factory workers generally receive **higher and cooler light levels** than office workers, largely due to skylights and greater daylight access, while office environments often remain below recommended illuminance and circadian thresholds for most of the workday. Light exposure varied significantly by **season, time of day, workstation location, and even viewing direction**, showing that standardized lighting guidelines often fail to reflect actual human experience. The findings highlight that occupant light exposure is difficult to predict and suggest that **more flexible, context-aware lighting design** is needed to better support health and circadian rhythms in real workplaces
