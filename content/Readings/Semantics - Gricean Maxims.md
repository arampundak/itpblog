Language is vague - “I’ll be there in a minute.” - and we understand it, we rely on **context, intention, and trust**.
When someone speaks to us, we automatically assume they’re trying to be truthful (**Quality**), they’re saying what matters (**Relevance**), they’re giving you the right amount (**Quantity**), they’re trying to be clear (**Manner**)
When a human violates a maxim its OK, when a machine does it - its not.

- gives too much info → feels annoying
- gives too little → feels dumb
- answers irrelevantly → feels useless
- answers ambiguously → feels untrustworthy

Summarize by LLM:

Paul Grice’s **Gricean Maxims** describe the unspoken rules that make everyday conversation work. Under his _Cooperative Principle_, speakers and listeners assume they are cooperating to communicate meaning. The four maxims are: **Quantity** (say as much as needed, no more), **Quality** (be truthful), **Relevance** (stay on topic), and **Manner** (be clear and unambiguous). When these maxims are followed, communication flows smoothly. When they are violated, communication breaks down—or, in some cases, meaning is _intentionally implied_ rather than stated outright (a “flout”), allowing people to communicate indirectly through sarcasm, humor, or implication.
