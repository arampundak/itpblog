**Calm Technology**
Connected to [[Little Signals]] project by Google (they also references this article in their website).
Calm technology means to operate in the background of our perception so we are more engaged with whats is happening around us, with the future us and with our past selves. It's related to "affordance" ([[Don Norman]]) - "design must be attuned to but not attended to".

"A calm technology will move easily from the periphery of our attention, to the center, and back."
"We must learn to design for the periphery so that we can most fully command technology without being dominated by it."

Natalie Jeremijenko - 'Dangaling String', a string connect to a motor operating each time a bit of information goes through an Ethernet cable - gives me an idea to do the same nowadays - what would an object like that will look like and what could viewers/users understand from it? #idea

This article argues that the most important technological shifts are not about new machines, but about how our relationship with technology changes over time—from shared mainframes, to personal computers, to networked systems, and now toward **ubiquitous computing**, where many embedded computers surround and serve each person invisibly. As computation spreads into everyday objects and environments, the central design challenge becomes **calm technology**: systems that inform us without overwhelming us. The authors propose designing technology that moves fluidly between the **center and the periphery of human attention**, allowing us to stay aware and in control without constant focus. Calm technologies enhance peripheral awareness, increase our sense of “locatedness” in the world, and support human agency rather than demanding attention—illustrated through examples like office windows, Internet multicast, and the “Dangling String.” Ultimately, the paper argues that as computers become ubiquitous, designing for calmness is essential to preserving human well-being, attention, and meaningful interaction in a deeply computational world.
