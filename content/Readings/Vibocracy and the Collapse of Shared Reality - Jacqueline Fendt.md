The text introduces **“vibocracy”** as a form of governance in which society is no longer driven by facts, evidence, or rational debate, but by emotional resonance and performative power. In a vibocracy, what matters is not what is true, but what _feels_ true and spreads virally; shame collapses, scandals become political assets, and legitimacy is produced through spectacle rather than effective institutions. Truth becomes fluid and short-lived, existing only as long as it dominates the feed, a condition reinforced by social media algorithms that reward emotional intensity over verification. Drawing on the idea of **neo-orality**, the text argues that digital culture revives a pre-literate, tribal mode of persuasion—where performance outweighs accuracy—turning politics into an ongoing emotional broadcast in which attention, not reality, is the primary source of power.
