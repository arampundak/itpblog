My thoughts:

I had the book at home and started reading it some time before class, revisiting the book now enlightened it with a new light - I highly enjoyed it and I love Munari's evolution from artist to designer.

**Preface pg.11**
Munari talks about art as it changes infront of his eyes from "old-fashioned categories of the visual arts" to new ways of creation and use of materials once looked down upon. He names the path that made the old categories obsolete. One example I love from that time is Rauschenberg & De Koning beef - with: 
>_**Erased de Kooning Drawing**_ is an early work of American artist [Robert Rauschenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rauschenberg "Robert Rauschenberg"), from 1953. This conceptual work presents an almost blank piece of paper in a gilded frame. It was created in 1953 when Rauschenberg erased a drawing he obtained from the [abstract expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_expressionist "Abstract expressionist") and Dutch-American artist [Willem de Kooning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Kooning "Willem de Kooning").

As he writes "The artist wants to make the viewer participate at all costs... he wants to sell his works of art in the chain stores just like any other commercial article" I cannot think of how contemporary this thought still is. The first part of the quote is as relevant as it was and the second will lead us to the establishment of the Designer of all of its fractures.
He continues to talk about "the desire to get back into society" and later on about how artist were servants of the elite. Since then designers made the same somersault. With todays starchitects and star designers being a part of the elite (boo! hyper capitalism sucks). 

"They have realized that at the present time subjective values are losing their importance in favor of objective values that can be understood by a greater number of people" - I deeply resonate with this observation, many movements have contributed to this evolution, 1789 comes first to mind. This is also connected to a text i've recently read by Mati Marianski - an Israeli publicist. He refers to [[Vibocracy and the Collapse of Shared Reality - Jacqueline Fendt]] and explain with these words:
>**“Vibocracy”** is a form of governance in which society is no longer driven by facts, evidence, or rational debate, but by emotional resonance and performative power. In a vibocracy, what matters is not what is true, but what _feels_ true and spreads virally; shame collapses, scandals become political assets, and legitimacy is produced through spectacle rather than effective institutions. Truth becomes fluid and short-lived, existing only as long as it dominates the feed, a condition reinforced by social media algorithms that reward emotional intensity over verification. Drawing on the idea of **neo-orality**, the text argues that digital culture revives a pre-literate, tribal mode of persuasion—where performance outweighs accuracy—turning politics into an ongoing emotional broadcast in which attention, not reality, is the primary source of power.

Ohh how I love and fear the "greater number of people".

And I cant finish this without a reference to one of Munari's collaborators and intellectual allies Enzo Mari with [[Autoprogettazione]] 

**Design as Art pg.25**
What I love most about this part is that the first paragraph does not hold the word "designer" and the second paragraph doesn't have the word "artist". Munari talkes about the artist interesting a selective group of people and that the paradigm must change and "become active as man among man". He talks about the virtues the new artist should hold - humility and competence, I want to add to that - empathy.
Later on he continuous with art should not divorce from life and artist/designers should make an impact with beautiful kitchen gadgets not compromised by chance or caprice, if only he would have known about shitty plastic products being manufactured by the trillions just to be thrown away (not disappear of course just dumped for later generations to deal with, where does all the trash goes to?). Additional though I had here is about minimalism in design.
He further talks about how  properly designed objects have the presence of an artist. Reminds me of [[Design as Participation - Kevin Slavin]].
The finishing paragraph talks about achieving a balanced life, until inequality will vanish - no such thing can happen to a greater number of people equally distributed.
