>Together, they make up an emerging landscape where many meaningful use cases haven’t yet been defined. Though they’ll share capabilities around intelligence and automation, each will need a distinct format that expresses its promise.

Robots will share a mind but will have different outposts, different use cases. 

>All these use cases will require different robotic formats... fit into the inherent social constructs these spaces embody. We need systems that move with the grain of human life, make people comfortable with their presence, and offer moments of surprise, delight, and personality.

>Home is one of the most complex human systems, full of rituals and meaning.

The home environment has a rhythm. Interaction is continuous, but has a bounding box. Different parts are used in different ways throughout different days. One might think a robotic system probe can recon a new household in the form of an autonomies vacuum, so later robotic entities are welcomed with knowledge of their surroundings and residents day agendas. #idea 

Making robots feel native to homes is a design problem, how will they look like? Our society's most notable robot, roaming as a silent butler when no one's around is the iRobot - form factored to go below sofas. A modern appliance: sleek, plasticy, minimalistic. 

**Robotic Anthropometry** - robots that can reach a table at +75cm can help in chores that smaller robots can't. 

>- What human patterns are at play?
>- How do people move, communicate, and collaborate within them?
>- What physical and social contexts shape our routines and expectations?
