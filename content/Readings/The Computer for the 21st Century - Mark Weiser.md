Mark Weiser argues that the most powerful technologies are those that **disappear into everyday life**, becoming so integrated that we stop noticing them. He proposes a shift from personal computing (one person, one machine) to **ubiquitous computing**, where many small, networked computers are embedded throughout the environment—tables, walls, paper, badges—quietly supporting human activity. Rather than focusing on screens and explicit interaction, Weiser introduces the idea of **embodied virtuality**, in which computation is woven into physical space and supports awareness through the **periphery of attention** rather than demanding constant focus. Using examples like tabs, pads, live boards, and the Active Badge system, he shows how technology can enhance coordination, context, and presence without overwhelming users. Ultimately, the paper argues that the future of computing should prioritize **calmness, invisibility, and human-centered design**, enabling people to remain more present, social, and empowered as computation becomes ubiquitous
