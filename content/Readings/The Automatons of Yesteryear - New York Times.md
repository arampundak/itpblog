>“In time to come the only work to be done by men and women will be that which requires faculties of discernment, discretion, and judgment,” Wensley was [quoted as saying in 1933](http://select.nytimes.com/gst/abstract.html?res=F40B16FA3D5516738DDDAE0994D1405B838FF1D3). “All other work – anything repetitive, routine, standardized – can better be done by machines.”

From Eric the Robot to Televox and Elektro, these machines weren’t impressive because of what they could do - they were impressive because of what people wanted them to be.

>The robotic brain will have to develop for years and years before the robotic body can merge with it.

And what about whats in between? the voice interfaces we are developing in the moment? How come an LLM have not yet been granted a physical form? and why is Alexa still not engaging with me on a personal level?

>Thomas A. Edison, a father figure to so many American inventors, when [he said](http://blog.library.si.edu/2011/02/to-invent-you-need-a-good-imagination-and-a-pile-of-junk/#.UgQeoGSAczQ) in 1915: “To invent, you need a good imagination and a pile of junk.”

He was itp.

Some more key point:
- human anxiety about replacement
- desire to create life
- discomfort when machines behave _almost_ human but not quite
- the early birth of the “uncanny valley,” before the term existed
