Zeynep Tufekci argues that making everyday objects “smart” by connecting them to the Internet introduces serious risks to security, safety, and privacy. Built on an insecure Internet infrastructure, networked objects—from cars to fridges—can be hacked, fail at scale, or expose vast amounts of personal data, turning convenience into danger. She warns that industries are adding complex software without the expertise or incentives to secure it properly, and argues that strong regulation, system isolation, and restraint about what truly needs connectivity are essential before the Internet of Things becomes a large-scale catastrophe.
