# Video brief 7: rods, not fish

Written for Itai: each scene says what the viewer must understand, what happens, the lines, and what the robot does. 

---

Research Question: What If the Shop Could Talk Back? How a Robot Can Be a Good Teacher in a Shared Makerspace
Degrees of Socratic teaching in human robot interaction

How much should a robot tell a newcomer in a shared makerspace, so they finish the job themselves, bring staff fewer but better questions, and leave confident they can do it again?

---
## What the film must prove (for the judges)
1. It happens in a real, shared shop, with other people around.
2. More than one person matters: user, person waiting, bystander, staff.
3. The robot's perception is visible: visor closed vs. open, docked vs. held.
4. A bystander can close the visor.
5. The robot hands off to staff.  

## The film in one paragraph 
Ari is a small robot that lives in a university makerspace (the ITP/IMA shop, 370 Jay St) and helps newcomers use the machines, mainly the laser cutter. It doesn't do the work; it helps you do it. How much it tells depends on what's at stake: on safety and procedure it tells you plainly, on your own decisions it asks you a question, and when you're stuck it offers options. Its camera sits behind a visor that's closed when you pick it up; you open it if you want Ari to see the machine. When you put it back on the dock, the visor closes and it's off. The film shows one student doing one laser job with Ari, from "I don't remember the steps" to explaining the machine to a friend, with Ari switched off in the background.

**The one sentence:** Ari teaches newcomers to use the machines themselves, so next time they don't need it.
**Closing line:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone."

## The idea the edit must make visible
Over the job, Ari moves from **telling** to **asking**. At the start (power, fumes, file setup) it tells. Later (a choice in the file) it asks. When something goes wrong it offers. If the viewer notices that shift, the film has explained the paper.

## Subtitles, slides, cards
- Dialogue is real audio, always subtitled. **Ari's lines in one accent color** (match the antennas or visor), people's in white. Bottom centre, one font. No speech bubbles.
- **Slides:** white background, one sentence, 4–7 seconds, ELEGNT-style.
- **Cards:** three words only, **Tells**, **Asks**, **Offers**, top-left, 1.5 s, the same every time. They appear the first time Ari does each.

## Scene by scene

### 1. Title (0:00–0:08)
- **Viewer understands:** a small robot with character.
- **What happens:** Ari on a white background, resting (head down, antennas down, visor closed). It lifts its head, turns to camera, small hello: head tilt, antenna wiggle. Cut to title, names, NYU logo.
- **Sound:** a short sting. Optional "hello" in Ari's voice.
- **Suggestions:** locked-off white sweep, soft light. Can be a separate session.
- **On screen:** title · "Aram Pundik · Aditya [surname] · NYU ITP" · NYU logo.

### 2. Busy shop (0:08–0:16)
- **Viewer understands:** a shared room, lots going on, staff busy. Nobody failing, just busy.
- **What happens:** evening shop footage, no dialogue. Staff helping someone at a bench, people waiting by the lasers (one with a restaurant buzzer, which the shop really uses for the laser queue), machines running. Ari on its dock somewhere in frame.
- **Suggestions:** 3–4 quick shots, one wide that holds staff + queue together. Ambient sound up.

### 3. Need + idea (0:16–0:28), two slides
- **Slide 1 (6 s):** "In a busy shop, not everyone gets help when they need it."
- **Slide 2 (6 s):** "Ari guides newcomers through the machines, and builds their confidence to do it alone." Below, three words in a row: **Tells** (safety) · **Asks** (your choices) · **Offers** (when you're stuck).

### 4. Hello (0:28–0:42)
- **Viewer understands:** the student is a beginner; Ari's goal is to make itself unnecessary. Lifting Ari wakes it.
- **What happens:** the student (call them **D**, for demonstrator) lifts Ari off the dock. Antennas rise, head comes up. Visor stays closed.
- **Dialogue:**
    - D: "Hi Ari. I'm new here. I used the laser once, but I don't remember the steps."
    - Ari: "Let's do it together, so next time you won't need me."
- **Suggestions:** close on hands lifting Ari and antennas rising (first time we see this), then a two-shot. D's line is the "before" of the bracket, so give it a clean, clear take.

### 5. Tells (0:42–1:00), card "Tells"
- **Viewer understands:** on safety and basics, Ari doesn't quiz you. It tells you.
- **What happens:** D doesn't know how to start the machine. Ari tells them where the power is and to turn on fume extraction. D does both.
- **Dialogue:**
    - D: "Where do I even turn it on?"
    - Ari: "Switch on the right side. And fume extraction on first, every time."
- **Note:** this is deliberately *not* a question. Safety is always direct in the paper. "Did you remember the fumes?" would be the wrong behavior.
- **Suggestions:** fast cuts: hand on the switch, the extraction turning on (sound!), Ari's head turning toward the machine.

### 6. The file (1:00–1:20), card "Asks"
- **Viewer understands:** on setup, Ari tells. On a choice that's D's, Ari asks, and D works it out.
- **What happens:** D and Ari look at the laptop together (Ari's head turned to the screen). Ari tells the cut-line setting (from the shop handbook: cut lines are no fill, 0.01 stroke, RGB red). Then Ari notices one shape has a fill and asks about it instead of fixing it.
- **Dialogue:**
    - Ari: "Cut lines: no fill, 0.01 stroke, red."
    - Ari: "This shape has a fill. What do you think the laser will do with it?"
    - D (pause): "...Engrave it? I don't want that." (removes the fill)
- **This is the moment of the film.** The shift from telling to asking happens here. Hold on D's face during the pause, 2 seconds.
- **Suggestions:** over-the-shoulder on the screen with Ari in frame; screen insert of the fill disappearing.

### 7. The material (1:20–1:40)
- **Viewer understands:** (a) consent: Ari's eye is closed until D opens it, and it sees only the machine; (b) Ari doesn't replace staff, it sends D to them with a better question.
- **What happens:** D holds up a sheet of thin plywood. Ari asks to see. D lifts the visor with a finger. Cut to Ari's view: the material and the bed only. Ari points D to the glue lines and to staff, because staff approve every material. D walks over to the staff member.
- **Dialogue:**
    - Ari: "Can I see it?" D: "Sure." (opens visor)
    - Ari: "See the dark lines between the layers? That's glue. Staff check every material, show them."
    - D (to staff): "Ari said to check the glue lines. These look light, is it okay?"
    - Staff (2 seconds): "Light is fine. Go ahead."
- **Grounding:** from the shop handbook: dark lines between plies mean industrial glue, which burns and gives off toxic fumes; staff must approve material before it goes in.
- **Suggestions:** the visor opening in macro is the hero shot of the film (and Figure 1 for the paper; shoot a still too). POV: Itai's camera, cropped tight, no faces. The staff exchange should feel quick and easy: that's the "fewer but better questions" point.

### 8. Offers (1:40–1:52), card "Offers"
- **Viewer understands:** when D is stuck, Ari gives choices; D still decides.
- **What happens:** the test cut doesn't go all the way through. Ari offers two options; D picks; the second test is clean.
- **Dialogue:** D: "It didn't cut through." Ari: "Two ways: go slower, or run a second pass. Your call." D: "Slower."
- **Suggestions:** close on the half-cut test piece, then the clean one. Staging the failed cut is fine; we disclose it.

### 9. Alone (1:52–2:00)
- **Viewer understands:** D finishes the job on their own. Ari has stopped talking.
- **What happens:** D sets up the final cut and runs it without asking anything. Ari watches, silent.
- **Suggestions:** no dialogue, ambient sound of the laser. A wide where D works and Ari is small in frame.

### 10a. Dock (2:00–2:06)
- **Viewer understands:** putting Ari back turns it off: it stops seeing and listening.
- **What happens:** D lifts the finished pieces out, puts Ari back on its dock. The visor closes and the antennas drop as it touches the dock.
- **Dialogue:** D: "Thanks, Ari." Ari: "I'm off."
- **Suggestions:** a clear close-up of the visor closing. It's the reset rule.

### 10b. Teaching a friend (2:06–2:22)
- **Viewer understands:** the "after." D can now do what they couldn't, and pass it on. Rods, not fish.
- **What happens:** a friend comes up to the laser, unsure. D shows them: power, fumes, "and check the glue lines." Ari sits on its dock in the background, antennas down, out of focus.
- **Dialogue:** Friend: "Do you know how this works?" D: "Yeah. Switch on the right, fumes on first..." (fade under)
- **Suggestions:** D in focus, Ari soft in the background. No line from Ari. This is the wow moment, and it works because Ari isn't in it.

### 11. How it works (2:22–2:37), slide + screen recording, *optional*
- **Viewer understands:** Ari answers instantly without being simplistic, and safety can't wait.
- **What happens:** split screen. Left: replay of scene 6. Right: animated diagram (same as the paper's architecture figure), two or three steps:
    1. "A realtime voice model answers at once."
    2. "A slower reasoning model reads the conversation and the shop's handbook, and passes a note to the next reply."
    3. "Safety rules live in the fast model, so a hazard never waits a turn."
- **Latency:** "Median response: [x] s" only if EXP 0 gives a number before the edit. No number, no claim. Don't say "solved."
- **Production:** Aditya, screen recording + motion graphics.

### 12. The shop, montage (2:37–2:49), *optional*
- **Viewer understands:** Ari serves the whole shop, and the visor rule applies to everyone.
- **What happens:** three clips, about 4 s each:
    1. Someone else lifts Ari off the dock; the visor is closed; they open it.
    2. 3D printing: "Which printer?" Ari: "The one by the window." They walk over.
    3. Small talk in the laser queue, buzzer in hand: "Long night?" Ari: "Third box this week."
- **Suggestions:** handheld, different corners of the shop.

### 13. Close (2:49–2:57), slide
- **Slide:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone."
- **Small text:** "Work in progress. Some behaviors puppeted; failed cut and dock staged."

## Rules for the edit
- Don't make staff look like they're failing. They're busy, and they answer D's better question in two seconds.
- D's hands do every step. Ari never touches a machine.
- Music only under the title, slides, scene 11 and the montage. Never under speech.
- If over 3:00, cut in this order: scene 12, then shorten 11 to captions 1 and 3, then shorten 2. Never cut 6, 7 or 10b.

## For the paper
- Visor rules shown: only the holder opens it; the dock closes it. Draft 7's abstract still says "anyone nearby can close" — update or keep as a feature not shown.
- Scene 7 shows the staff-approval rule from the handbook. That's a good concrete example for the "prepared handoffs" contribution.

## For the shoot
- **Cast:** D, the staff member (a real one if possible), the friend, 2 people in the queue (can double as montage people). Releases for everyone visible.
- **Props:** laptop with a box file (one shape with a fill), thin plywood with light glue lines, a failed test piece and a clean one, restaurant buzzer, a 3D print (montage).
- **Puppeting, off camera:** lift-to-wake, head turns (to the screen, the machine, D), the title hello, and the visor close + antennas down on the dock.
- **Audio:** lav on D, staff and friend; Ari's voice recorded clean from the laptop.
- **Stills for the paper:** visor opening (Fig. 1), Ari's POV of the material, the busy-shop wide, the friend scene with Ari docked.
- **Shoot order:** scenes 4–10a as one continuous session at the laser (cut up later), then 10b, then the shop footage and montage, then the white sweep separately.
