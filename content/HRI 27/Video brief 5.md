# Video brief 5: for Itai's shot list

Replaces [[Video brief 4]]. Written for Itai, so each scene says what the viewer must understand, what happens, who says what, and what the robot does. The shot list is his: framing and coverage below are suggestions, not instructions. Look, sound and the shoot checklist from [[Video brief 2]] still apply.

## What changed from Brief 4
- **Explainer slides.** ELEGNT mixes live scenes with short written explanation slides. We do the same: four slides carry what the scenes can't (the problem, the idea, consent, how it works).
- **Dialogue stays audible, subtitled in two colors.** Ari's words in one color, people's in white. No comic bubbles (reasons below).
- **The visor starts closed and the student opens it to give consent.** No one closes it at the end. The bystander-closes beat is cut.
- More explanation per scene for Itai.

## The film in one paragraph (read this first, Itai)
Ari is a small robot that lives in a university makerspace (the ITP/IMA shop, 370 Jay St) and helps newcomers use the laser cutter. Its job is to teach, not to do: it tells a student plainly what's dangerous, asks questions when the decision is hers, and offers options when she's stuck. We call that "degrees of Socraticness," and it's the main idea of the paper. Ari's camera sits behind a visor that is closed by default; the student opens it only when Ari needs to see the laser bed. The film follows one student through one laser job, from "I don't know where to start" to holding her finished piece, with short slides in between that explain what you just saw.

**The one sentence:** a robot in a shared makerspace should tell newcomers just enough that they finish the job themselves, without taking their place or watching them.
**Closing line:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone."

## Subtitles: two colors
**Recommendation: two-color subtitles.** Ari's lines in one accent color (match the robot's antenna or the visor, so the color *is* Ari), people's lines in white. Same font, same position (bottom centre), always on.
Bubbles are fine once, as a joke, in the title (Ari's "hello"). Nowhere else.

## Look of the slides
White background, same as the title shot. One sentence each, maybe a simple diagram, one plain typeface. 4–8 seconds each. They should feel like ELEGNT's text slides, not a lecture: if a slide needs more than one sentence, the scene before it didn't do its job.

## Scene by scene

### 1. Title (0:00–0:10)
- **Viewer understands:** a small robot with character. Nothing about the shop yet.
- **What happens:** Ari sits on a white background, dormant (head down, antennas down). It wakes, lifts its head, turns to camera and does a small hello (head tilt, antenna wiggle). Add the title, names, NYU logo.
- **Robot:** wake + hello motion, puppeted or scripted. Visor closed.
- **Sound:** quiet, maybe a short music sting. Optional "hello" in Ari's voice, in a single comic bubble (the only bubble in the film).
- **Suggestions:** locked-off, white sweep, soft even light. Can be shot on any day, separately from the shop.
- **On screen:** title, "Aram Pundik · Aditya De · NYU ITP", NYU logo.

### 2. The problem (0:10–0:28)
- **Viewer understands:** a newcomer is unsure at the laser cutter; the staff member is busy; the queue exists. Staff aren't failing, there's just one of them.
- **What happens:** evening in the shop. A staff member is helping another student. Two people wait near the lasers. Our student (S) stands at the laser with a file on a laptop and a sheet of plywood, clearly unsure. She glances at her phone, puts it away. She catches the staff member's eye. He points across without stopping what he's doing.
- **Dialogue:** Staff: "Talk to Ari." (white)
- **Then slide (white, 6 s):** "Staff answer the same laser questions all night. A phone would just tell her which button to press."
- **Robot:** on its dock, out of focus in the background or not yet seen.
- **Suggestions:** a wide that holds staff, queue and S together; a close on S's hands and the plywood. The staff member should look busy and kind, not absent.
- **Note:** this is our experience as shop staff, not a study result. Nothing here may look like data.

### 3. The idea (0:28–0:36), slide
- **Viewer understands:** the whole design principle in one look.
- **Slide:** "Ari is a Socratic tutor. It tells you more or less depending on what's at stake." Underneath, three words in a row with a small icon each: **Direct** (safety) · **Asking** (design) · **Offering** (stuck). These three words return later as scene cards, so the viewer can follow along.

### 4. Waking (0:36–0:50)
- **Viewer understands:** Ari is asleep until you pick it up. Holding it means you're the one it's helping.
- **What happens:** S walks to the dock and lifts Ari. Its antennas rise and it turns toward her.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "Hi. What are you making?" S: "A box. I've never cut before."
- **Robot:** lift-to-wake (antennas up, head up). Visor stays closed.
- **Suggestions:** close on hands lifting, then a two-shot. This is where the viewer meets the antennas, so give them a clear close-up.

### 5. Direct (0:50–1:10), card "Direct"
- **Viewer understands:** when something can hurt you or the machine, Ari doesn't ask questions. It tells you.
- **What happens:** S puts Ari down beside the laser. She reaches to start the job. Ari stops her and walks her through the steps; she does each one with her own hands.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "Before you start: air assist on, and the lid stays closed while it cuts." "Now focus the laser on the material." "And you stay with the machine the whole time." S: "Got it."
- **Robot:** head turns between the machine and her. Visor closed (it doesn't need to see for this).
- **Suggestions:** over-the-shoulder on the machine with Ari at the edge of frame; close-ups of her hands on the buttons. Ari never touches anything.

### 6. Consent (1:10–1:28), card "Consent"
- **Viewer understands:** Ari's camera is closed by default. It asks to see, she decides, and when it sees, it sees only the laser bed.
- **What happens:** S is about to choose settings. Ari asks to see. She looks at the visor, then lifts it open with her finger.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "Can I see the bed?" S: "Sure." Ari: "Thanks. I only see the machine."
- **Insert:** cut to Ari's point of view: a tight, cropped view of the laser bed, no faces, nothing else in the room.
- **Then slide (5 s):** "The camera is behind a visor. It starts closed. Only the person holding Ari opens it."
- **Robot:** visor opens (by her hand). Head tilts toward the bed.
- **Suggestions:** macro on the visor opening is the hero shot of the film (it's also Figure 1 for the paper, so shoot a still). The POV can be faked with Itai's camera, cropped tight.

### 7. Asking (1:28–1:55), card "Asking". Wow moment.
- **Viewer understands:** on a decision that's hers, Ari asks instead of answering, and that makes her think.
- **What happens:** S asks for the settings. Ari asks back. She pauses, thinks, and decides to run a small test cut in the corner first.
- **Dialogue:** S: "What settings should I use?" Ari: "What happens if the first cut is wrong?" S (pause): "...I lose the sheet. I'll test in the corner first." Ari: "Good idea."
- **Robot:** a small head tilt on the question, then stillness while she thinks.
- **Suggestions:** close two-shot, then hold on **her face** during the pause. The robot is not the hero of this shot; her thinking is. Let the silence run 2–3 seconds.

### 8. Offering (1:55–2:12), card "Offering"
- **Viewer understands:** when she's genuinely stuck, Ari gives her options, and she still makes the choice.
- **What happens:** the test cut doesn't go all the way through. She looks at it, frustrated. Ari offers two options; she picks one; it works.
- **Dialogue:** S: "It didn't cut through." Ari: "Two ways to go: slow it down, or run a second pass. Which one?" S: "Slower." (test cut, success)
- **Robot:** looks at the test piece (visor still open, POV insert optional), then at her.
- **Suggestions:** close on the half-cut test piece, then the clean one. Staging the failed cut is fine; we disclose it at the end.

### 9. How it works (2:12–2:35), slide + screen recording
- **Viewer understands:** why Ari answers instantly without being simplistic, and that safety rules can't wait.
- **What happens:** split screen. Left: a replay of a moment from scene 7 or 8. Right: an animated diagram (the same one as the paper's architecture figure) builds in three steps:
    1. "A realtime voice model answers at once."
    2. "A slower reasoning model reads the conversation and the shop's handbook, and passes a note to the next reply."
    3. "Safety rules live in the fast model, so a hazard never waits a turn."
- **Latency:** add "Median response: [x] s" only if EXP 0 gives us a number before the edit. No number, no claim. Don't use the word "solved."
- **Sound:** low music, no dialogue.
- **Production:** Aditya does the screen recording and the diagram animation. Not a camera job.

### 10. Handing over (2:35–2:45), card "Handing over"
- **Viewer understands:** Ari doesn't approve anything. Staff do.
- **What happens:** the staff member from scene 2 comes over. Ari hands over, quietly.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "She tested first. She's ready for you to check." Staff: "Let's see."
- **Suggestions:** a wide that brings staff back into the frame. Same staff member as scene 2, so the film closes the loop.

### 11. Ending (2:45–3:00)
- **Viewer understands:** she did it herself.
- **What happens:** the laser finishes. S lifts out the cut pieces and holds them. She puts Ari back on its dock; its antennas lower and it rests. We don't show the visor closing.
- **Then slide:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone." Small text below: "Work in progress. Some behaviors puppeted; failed cut staged."
- **Suggestions:** hold on the pieces in her hands, then Ari resting in the background, out of focus.

## Rules for the edit
- Dialogue is real audio, always subtitled. Ari's subtitles in the accent color, people's in white.
- Scene cards (Direct, Consent, Asking, Offering, Handing over): one word, top-left, 1.5 s, same each time.
- Slides: white, one sentence. If a slide needs two, fix the scene.
- Music only under the title, slide 3 and scene 9. Never under speech.
- Don't make staff look like they're failing.
- If over 3:00, cut in this order: shorten 9 to captions 1 and 3; drop slide in 6; shorten 10. Never cut 6, 7 or 11.
