# Video brief 3: what it really does, one card at a time

Replaces [[Video brief 2]] for structure. Look and sound, the paper stills, and the shoot checklist from Brief 2 still apply.
Inputs: Itai's notes (08/11, transcription below), ELEGNT as the structural model, [[Paper brief]] message.


How much should a robot tell a newcomer in a shared makerspace, so they finish the job themselves, bring staff fewer but better questions, and leave confident they can do it again?

What If the Shop Could Talk Back? How a Robot Can Be a Good Teacher in a Shared Makerspace
Degrees of Socratic teaching in human robot interaction

## One sentence (from the [[Paper brief]])
A robot in a shared space should help people do what they didn't think they could, without taking their place or watching them.
Every shot has to show one half of that sentence: **helps her do it herself**, or **doesn't watch / doesn't take over**. If a shot shows neither, cut it.

## Model: ELEGNT (Apple, 2025)
ELEGNT walks the lamp through short task scenarios, each one labeled, with no voiceover telling you what to think. That's the format: **informative, no narrative arc to sell**. A title card names the behavior, the scene shows it, then it moves on.
What we change: ELEGNT is one person at a desk. Our claim is about the room, so most cards include a second person (waiting, neighbor, staff).
What we drop from ELEGNT: the functional vs. expressive comparison. We don't have a comparison condition and we can't claim one (see "Claims we may NOT make" in the Paper brief).

## How Itai's notes fit in
- **One use case, the core workflow:** a single laser job, from file to finished piece. The cards follow that order, so the workflow *is* the structure.
- **What it really does:** two cards (Guiding, Asking) show the actual tutoring. Before, the privacy states took up almost all the time.
- **Wow moment:** the Asking card. She asks for settings; Ari asks back; she decides on a test cut. Hold on her face, not the robot.
- **Shop manager says "talk to Ari":** the cold open, one line. It also shows that staff send people to the robot, not the other way round.
- **Simply Piano A → B → C:** kept only as a frame. Stuck in the first shot, piece in hand in the last. No story beats in between, only cards.
- **Different people, each one's process:** the montage at the end.

## Rules for the edit
- Title cards: one plain typeface, one word or two, same position every time. Nothing else on screen except captions.
- No voiceover, no music under speech. Music only in the montage, or none.
- Each card is one behavior. If a scene needs explaining, it's two cards or a bad scene.
- The person's hands do the work in every card. Ari never touches the machine.
