# Video brief 6: one hero, then the shop

Replaces [[Video brief 5]]. Written for Itai, so each scene says what the viewer must understand, what happens, who says what, and what the robot does. Framing and coverage are suggestions; the shot list is his. Look, sound and the shoot checklist from [[Video brief 2]] still apply.

## Decisions since Brief 5 (13/11, Aram)
- **One hero + montage.** One student does one laser job start to finish. Then a montage of other people who come to Ari with something they need.
- **Keep the need.** Ari is an addition to the shop, not a fix for failing staff: it takes the repeated questions so staff time goes to judgment.
- **The visor resets on the dock.** Every new person finds it closed and opens it themselves (consent). Putting Ari back on the dock closes the visor and lowers the antennas: "I'm off."
- **3D printing:** one 5-second clip in the montage, only to show that Ari knows the shop.
- **Wow moment:** she arrives stressed; Ari steadies her and then asks a question she can answer; she realizes she knows more than she thought.
- **Waking** is no longer its own scene. Lifting Ari is a two-second action inside scene 4.

## The film in one paragraph (read this first, Itai)
Ari is a small robot that lives in a university makerspace (the ITP/IMA shop, 370 Jay St) and helps newcomers use the machines, mainly the laser cutter. Its job is to teach, not to do: it tells you plainly what's dangerous, asks questions when the decision is yours, and offers options when you're stuck. That idea, "degrees of Socraticness," is the core of the paper. Its camera sits behind a visor that's always closed when you pick it up; you open it if you want Ari to see the machine, and it sees only the machine. When you put Ari back on its dock, the visor closes and it switches off. The film follows one stressed student through her first laser cut, then shows the shop: other people coming to Ari with their own small problems.

**The one sentence:** a robot in a shared makerspace should tell newcomers just enough that they finish the job themselves, without taking their place or watching them.
**Closing line:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone."

## Subtitles and slides (unchanged from Brief 5)
- Dialogue is real audio, always subtitled. **Ari's lines in one accent color** (match the antennas or visor), people's in white. Bottom centre, one font. No speech bubbles, except optionally Ari's "hello" in the title.
- **Slides:** white background, one sentence, 4–7 seconds, ELEGNT-style. If a slide needs two sentences, the scene before it didn't do its job.
- **Scene cards** (Consent, Direct, Asking, Offering, Handing over): one word, top-left, 1.5 s, the same every time.

## Scene by scene

### 1. Title (0:00–0:08)
- **Viewer understands:** a small robot with character. Nothing about the shop yet.
- **What happens:** Ari on a white background, resting (head down, antennas down, visor closed). It lifts its head, turns to camera and does a small hello: head tilt, antenna wiggle. Cut to the title, names, NYU logo.
- **Sound:** quiet; a short sting. Optional "hello" in Ari's voice.
- **Suggestions:** locked-off, white sweep, soft even light. Shoot on a separate day if easier.
- **On screen:** title · "Aram Pundik · Aditya [surname] · NYU ITP" · NYU logo.

### 2. The need (0:08–0:24)
- **Viewer understands:** the shop is busy and shared; the staff member is good at his job and has too many people; the questions repeat. Nobody is failing.
- **What happens:** evening in the shop. The staff member is helping a student at a bench. Two people wait by the lasers (one holding a restaurant buzzer, which the shop really uses for the laser queue). Our hero, **S**, walks in with a laptop and a sheet of plywood, looks at the laser, looks at the staff member, hesitates. He sees her, smiles, points across the room without stopping.
- **Dialogue:** Staff: "Talk to Ari, I'll come check before you cut." (white)
- **Slide (6 s):** "Staff answer the same questions all night. Ari takes the repeated ones, so staff time goes to the hard ones."
- **Suggestions:** one wide holding staff, queue and S together; a close on the buzzer; a close on S's face as she hesitates. The staff member should look busy and kind.
- **Note:** this comes from our experience as shop staff, not a study. Nothing should look like data.

### 3. The idea (0:24–0:30), slide
- **Viewer understands:** the design principle at a glance, so they can follow the scenes.
- **Slide:** "Ari is a Socratic tutor. It tells you more or less depending on what's at stake." Below, three words in a row: **Direct** (safety) · **Asking** (your decisions) · **Offering** (when you're stuck). These come back as scene cards.

### 4. Consent (0:30–0:50), card "Consent"
- **Viewer understands:** Ari wakes when you lift it; its eye starts closed; you decide to open it; it sees only the machine. Also: S is stressed.
- **What happens:** S lifts Ari off the dock. Antennas rise, head comes up (2 seconds, this replaces the old Waking scene). She sets it beside the laser. Ari asks to see; she lifts the visor with one finger.
- **Dialogue:**
    - Ari: "Hi. What are we cutting?"
    - S: "A box. For critique tomorrow. I've never used this."
    - Ari: "Then we'll go one step at a time. Can I see the machine?"
    - S: "...Sure." (opens visor)
    - Ari: "Thanks. I only see the bed."
- **Insert:** Ari's point of view: a tight, cropped view of the laser bed. No faces, nothing else in the room.
- **Slide (5 s):** "Ari's eye starts closed. Only the person holding it opens it."
- **Suggestions:** macro on the visor opening is the hero shot of the film (and Figure 1 for the paper, so shoot a still too). The POV can be Itai's camera, cropped tight. Let S's stress read in her voice and posture, not in the lines.

### 5. Direct (0:50–1:05), card "Direct"
- **Viewer understands:** when something can hurt you or the machine, Ari doesn't ask. It tells you.
- **What happens:** S reaches for the start button. Ari stops her and gives the steps; she does each one with her own hands.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "Wait, two things first. Air assist on. And you stay with the machine the whole time it cuts." S: "Okay." (does both)
- **Robot:** head turns between machine and S.
- **Suggestions:** over-the-shoulder on the control panel, Ari at the edge of frame; closes on her hands. Ari never touches anything.

### 6. Asking (1:05–1:30), card "Asking". The wow moment.
- **Viewer understands:** Ari doesn't hand her the answer. It asks a question she can answer, and she discovers she already knows. Her stress drops.
- **What happens:** S wants the settings. Ari asks back. She thinks, answers, and decides on a test cut. A small visible change: shoulders down, a half smile.
- **Dialogue:**
    - S: "Just tell me the settings, I don't want to ruin the sheet."
    - Ari: "Fair. What would you do if you weren't sure a cut would work?"
    - S (pause): "...Try it small first. In the corner."
    - Ari: "That's exactly what staff would do."
- **Robot:** a small head tilt on the question, then still while she thinks.
- **Suggestions:** close two-shot, then **hold on her face** during the pause; let it run 2–3 seconds. Her thinking is the hero of the shot, not the robot.

### 7. Offering (1:30–1:45), card "Offering"
- **Viewer understands:** when she's genuinely stuck, Ari gives choices and she still decides.
- **What happens:** the test cut doesn't go all the way through. S frowns at it. Ari offers two options; she picks one; the second test is clean.
- **Dialogue:** S: "It didn't cut through." Ari: "Two ways: go slower, or run a second pass. Your call." S: "Slower."
- **Suggestions:** close on the half-cut test piece, then the clean one. Staging the failed cut is fine; we disclose it.

### 8. Handing over (1:45–1:53), card "Handing over"
- **Viewer understands:** Ari doesn't approve anything. Staff do.
- **What happens:** the staff member from scene 2 comes over, as promised.
- **Dialogue:** Ari: "She tested first. Ready for you to check." Staff: "Looks good. Go."
- **Suggestions:** a wide that brings the staff member back into frame; same person as scene 2, closing the loop.

### 9. Done (1:53–2:05)
- **Viewer understands:** she did it herself. And when Ari goes back on the dock, it stops seeing and listening.
- **What happens:** the laser finishes. S lifts out the cut pieces and holds them up, relieved. She puts Ari back on its dock. The visor closes, the antennas lower.
- **Dialogue:** S: "Thanks." Ari: "You did it. I'm off."
- **Robot:** visor closes and antennas drop **as it touches the dock**. This is the reset rule, so give it a clear close-up.
- **Suggestions:** hold on the pieces in her hands; then a close on the dock as the visor shuts.

### 10. How it works (2:05–2:25), slide + screen recording
- **Viewer understands:** Ari answers instantly without being simplistic, and safety rules can't wait.
- **What happens:** split screen. Left: a replay of scene 6. Right: an animated diagram (the same as the paper's architecture figure) builds in three steps:
    1. "A realtime voice model answers at once."
    2. "A slower reasoning model reads the conversation and the shop's handbook, and passes a note to the next reply."
    3. "Safety rules live in the fast model, so a hazard never waits a turn."
- **Latency:** add "Median response: [x] s" only if EXP 0 gives a number before the edit. No number, no claim. Don't say "solved."
- **Sound:** low music, no dialogue.
- **Production:** Aditya does the screen recording and diagram animation. Not a camera job.

### 11. The shop, montage (2:25–2:50)
- **Viewer understands:** Ari belongs to the shop, not to one person. Different people come to it with their own small problem and leave having solved it themselves. The visor rule repeats.
- **What happens:** four short clips, 5–7 s each. Each one starts with a need and ends with the person acting.
    1. **Visor, again (7 s).** A second student lifts Ari off the dock. The visor is closed. She opens it herself. Holds up a sheet of plastic. Ari: "Read me the label first." She turns the sheet and reads it.
    2. **3D printing (5 s).** Someone holding a failed print. "Which printer should I use?" Ari: "The one by the window. And check the bed is clean." He's already walking over.
    3. **Small talk (6 s).** Someone in the laser queue, buzzer in hand, chatting with Ari while they wait. "Long night?" Ari: "Third box this week. Yours is next."
    4. **Back on the dock (5 s).** Someone sets Ari down. Visor closes, antennas drop. Ari: "I'm off." They walk away to their machine.
- **Note:** Ari doesn't "solve" anyone's problem for them. In every clip the person does the next step.
- **Suggestions:** handheld is fine here; different corners of the shop; at least one wide where several people work while Ari sits on its dock in the background. Music can come in under the montage, but drop it under dialogue.

### 12. Close (2:50–3:00), slide
- **Slide:** "The measure of a teaching robot is what people can do when it's gone."
- **Small text below:** "Work in progress. Some behaviors puppeted; failed cut and dock staged."

## Rules for the edit
- Don't make staff look like they're failing. They send people to Ari and they come back to check.
- People's hands do every step. Ari never touches a machine.
- Music only under the title, slide 3, scene 10 and the montage. Never under speech.
- If over 3:00, cut in this order: shorten 10 to captions 1 and 3; drop montage clip 3; shorten 8. Never cut 4, 6 or 9.

## For the paper
- The film now shows **two visor rules**: only the holder opens it; returning to the dock closes it. Draft 7's abstract says "a visor anyone nearby can close," which the film doesn't show. Add the dock reset to the design section, and either keep "anyone can close" as a feature not shown, or cut it.
- 3D printing appears for 5 seconds. The paper's one sentence on it is enough.
