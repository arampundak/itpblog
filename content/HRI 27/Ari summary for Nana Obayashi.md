# What if the shop could talk back?
Aram Pundik, ITP (NYU Tisch) · with Aditya De · HRI 2027 Student Design Competition, "Robots in Public Spaces" (due Nov 20)

**The situation.** Makerspaces put irreversible machines in front of people who have rarely used them. When staff are busy, a newcomer at the laser cutter can wait, guess, or ask a phone, which answers instantly and teaches little.

**The design.** Ari is a small tutor robot built on Pollen Robotics' open-source Reachy Mini, for the shared ITP shop. It is direct about safety and irreversible steps, and asks questions about design decisions, so the decisions stay with the student. It hands every approval to shop staff.

**The question.** A robot in a shared room is never only one person's: it belongs to the institution, and it can see everyone nearby. We ask how a robot can carry a space's social contract to newcomers without becoming the institution's eyes on everyone in the room.

**Our answer starts with the body.** Ari's perception is public and physical rather than promised in software. Its antennas rise when it listens. A visor covers its camera, and when raised it frames only the machine bed. Only the person holding Ari can open the visor; anyone nearby can close it. Ari keeps no images or names, and will not report who did what.

**Status.** Working voice tutor on Reachy Mini, grounded in the shop's staff handbook. User journey enacted twice in the shop. Form mockups. Next two weeks: visor, listening signal, a safety self-test, and a film in two NYU makerspaces.

**Why I'm writing to you.** Your work treats a robot's body as part of how it thinks. Ari applies a similar idea socially: the body is where people can see, and limit, what a robot perceives. I'd value 20 minutes on:
1. Designing a visor that anyone can push closed safely, mechanically or with compliant materials.
2. Whether "privacy from the body up" holds up from an embodied-intelligence point of view.
3. Who at Tandon works on human-robot interaction and might give feedback.
