# Video brief: "What if the shop could talk back?"

## The idea in one breath
A small robot lives in a university shop and helps newcomers use the laser cutter. The film isn't about the robot being smart. It's about **the room**: everyone in the shop can tell what the robot sees, hears, and who it's paying attention to, and anyone can shut its eye.

## What the film must prove (for the judges)
1. It happens in a real, shared shop, with other people around.
2. More than one person matters: user, person waiting, bystander, staff.
3. The robot's perception is visible: visor closed vs. open, docked vs. held.
4. A bystander can close the visor.
5. The robot hands off to staff.
Length: max 3:00. Captions on all dialogue (shop is loud; also accessibility). Disclose puppeted parts on screen.

## Two visual devices
- **Ari's POV.** Cut to what Ari's camera sees. Visor closed = black frame with sound only. Visor open = a tight, cropped view of the laser bed, no faces. Viewers learn the rules by seeing them.
- **Wide shots that hold the room.** Most robot videos are close-ups of the robot. Ours needs frames where the robot, the user, and the person waiting are all visible at once. The claim is about the room.

## Tone and look
Observational, not an ad: calm handheld or tripod, real shop light and sound, no stock music bed under dialogue. Warm, a bit funny (Ari has some attitude). The robot should feel like it belongs to the shop, not like a gadget placed in it.

## References to watch together
- **Luxo Jr. (Pixar, 1986):** a simple object with no face reads as alive through timing and head direction alone. That's Ari's body language.
- **ELEGNT (Apple, 2025):** a lamp robot whose movement is both functional and expressive; watch how they frame the object and the person together. Search "Apple ELEGNT robot lamp video".
- **Teenage Engineering product films:** clean, honest object shots. Useful for the dock and visor close-ups.

## Logistics to settle with him
- Two shoot days: Nov 14–15 (ITP shop, then second makerspace). Off-peak hours so the shop is safe and quiet enough.
- Shop permission from staff; the laser runs only with staff present.
- Release forms for **everyone** on camera, including background people. No real students who didn't sign.
- Cast: user, waiting student, neighbor, staff member (a real staff member would be best).
- Sound: lav on the user, a separate clean recording of Ari's voice (or capture it from the laptop).
- Puppeting: who triggers Ari's head turn and lift-wake off-camera.
- Deliverables: 3-minute cut by **Nov 18**; also 3–4 stills for paper figures (hero shot = Fig. 1).
- Questions for him: How many setups can we do per day? Does he have a macro lens and a lav kit? Can he shoot the POV shots from the robot's camera, or fake them with his own?
