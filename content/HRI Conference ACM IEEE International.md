**Student Design Competition (SDC), HRI 2027: summary**

**What it is:** A showcase track where student teams present HRI design work. Judges care about design thinking, meaning the interaction, the reasoning behind it, and how it holds up in real deployment. Technical sophistication counts for little. It's reviewed less strictly than full papers.

**Who can enter:**

- Teams of 2–5 students, from undergrad to PhD, in any discipline.
- Teammates can be at different institutions, but each student can be on only one team.
- Faculty can be co-authors, but students have to lead and be listed first.
- The chairs will help match solo applicants into teams.

**Theme: robots in public spaces.** This means real shared settings like sidewalks, museums, airports and hospital lobbies. To count as on-theme, your entry has to:

- Name a specific setting.
- Account for more than one party, including bystanders.
- Make design choices that only make sense because the space is public.
- Deal with at least one real problem in that space, such as crowding, noise, accessibility or consent.

**Accepted formats:** Physical robots (new or modified), software-only systems, interaction concepts (Wizard-of-Oz, enactment, video prototypes), or spatial/service design. Lo-fi or puppeted robots are fine if you say so. Expensive hardware gives no advantage.

**Submission:**

- A paper of up to 4 pages including references, in ACM format. It is not anonymous.
- It's published as a non-archival companion paper.
- An optional video of up to 3 minutes is strongly encouraged.
- The paper must cover: setting, stakeholders, the interaction, goals and constraints, process and rationale (including alternatives you considered), expected user experience and failure modes, a demo plan, and at least one image.
- Unfinished work is allowed. You have to show what's done, what remains, and that it's realistic, and it must be complete by camera-ready.
- You can't submit the same project to both the SDC and Late-Breaking Reports.

**Judging:** Scenario clarity, handling of multiple parties and bystanders, depth of rationale, ethics and accessibility, how well the demo communicates, and whether the remaining work is feasible.

**Awards:** Best Design, Public Spaces, People's Choice.

**At the conference:** At least one team member must attend in person and demo live or with video support. Each team brings something physical, or a poster if travel makes that impossible.

**Funding:** Not guaranteed. They point to IEEE RAS travel grants, student volunteering (which waives registration), Pioneers, and your school's scholarships.

**What this means for you:** As an ITP student you're eligible, but you can't enter alone, so you'd need at least one teammate. Your expressive-motion work could fit if you place it in a specific public setting, for example a low-DoF robot signaling attentiveness to both the person it's serving and bystanders in a museum or lobby. That would also be a lighter lift than a full paper. The text doesn't say whether the SDC and Pioneers can overlap. It only rules out the SDC plus Late-Breaking Reports combination, so check with the chairs before submitting to both.
